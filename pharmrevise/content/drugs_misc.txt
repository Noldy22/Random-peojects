# Drug monographs — obstetrics/reproductive, haematology, musculoskeletal, urology, antidotes & emergency, allergy, dermatology, oncology.

=== drug: oxytocin
name: Oxytocin
class: Uterotonic (posterior pituitary hormone)
group: Obstetric
nemlit: A (injection 10 IU/mL)
forms: Injection 10 IU/mL (1 mL ampoule)
moa: Stimulates oxytocin receptors in myometrium → rhythmic contractions; also milk ejection.
uses:
- Active management of the third stage of labour (prevention of PPH) — drug of choice
- Treatment of postpartum haemorrhage; induction/augmentation of labour; incomplete abortion
dose:
- AMTSL: 10 IU IM within 1 minute of delivery of the baby
- PPH treatment: 10 IU IV/IM, then 20–40 IU in 1 L normal saline/Ringer's at 60 drops/min
- Induction: 2.5 IU in 500 mL saline, start 10 drops/min, increase by 10 drops every 30 min until 3 contractions in 10 min lasting >40 s
ci:
- Obstructed labour, fetal distress, cephalopelvic disproportion, malpresentation, placenta praevia (for induction)
- Hypertonic uterine contractions
se:
- Uterine hyperstimulation, fetal distress, uterine rupture
- Water intoxication/hyponatraemia (large volumes of dextrose, high doses), hypotension with rapid IV bolus
ddi:
- Prostaglandins (misoprostol) :: Potentiate uterotonic effect → hyperstimulation :: Avoid simultaneous use for induction
food:
- Not applicable
preg: Used only in labour/postpartum.
lact: Compatible.
monitor:
- Contractions and fetal heart rate every 30 min during infusion; uterine tone and blood loss postpartum; fluid balance
advice:
- Explain purpose of injection after birth (prevents heavy bleeding)
storage: Cold chain 2–8°C (heat-sensitive — loses potency); do not freeze.
pearls:
- Heat-stable carbetocin is an alternative where cold chain is poor

=== drug: misoprostol
name: Misoprostol
class: Prostaglandin E1 analogue
group: Obstetric
nemlit: A (tablet 200 mcg)
forms: Tablet 200 mcg
moa: PGE1 agonist → uterine contraction and cervical ripening; reduces gastric acid and increases mucus.
uses:
- Prevention of PPH where oxytocin is unavailable; treatment of PPH (adjunct)
- Incomplete abortion/miscarriage management; cervical ripening and induction (specialist, low dose)
- NSAID ulcer prevention (rarely used)
dose:
- PPH prevention: 600 mcg orally once after delivery of the baby
- PPH treatment: 800 mcg sublingually
- Incomplete abortion (<13 weeks): 600 mcg orally or 400 mcg sublingually single dose
ci:
- Pregnancy where continuation is desired (abortifacient, teratogenic — Möbius syndrome)
- Previous caesarean scar for induction at term (rupture risk)
se:
- Shivering, fever (transient), diarrhoea, nausea, abdominal cramps
- Uterine hyperstimulation/rupture
ddi:
- Oxytocin :: Additive effect :: Do not give both simultaneously for induction
- Magnesium-containing antacids :: Worsen diarrhoea :: Avoid
food:
- Food :: Reduces diarrhoea (oral use for ulcer) :: —
preg: Contraindicated in desired pregnancy.
lact: Compatible after single postpartum dose.
monitor:
- Bleeding, temperature, uterine tone
advice:
- Shivering and fever after the dose are common and settle within hours
storage: Below 25°C, keep dry in blister (moisture degrades it).
pearls:
- Community-based distribution for home births reduces PPH

=== drug: magnesium-sulfate
name: Magnesium sulfate
class: Anticonvulsant (for eclampsia); electrolyte
group: Obstetric
nemlit: A (injection 50% — 500 mg/mL in 10 mL)
forms: Injection 50% (5 g/10 mL); 20% for IV use
moa: Calcium antagonist at NMDA receptors and neuromuscular junction; cerebral vasodilator — raises seizure threshold.
uses:
- Prevention and treatment of seizures in severe pre-eclampsia and eclampsia (drug of choice)
- Fetal neuroprotection before preterm birth <32 weeks
- Acute severe asthma (single IV dose); torsades de pointes; hypomagnesaemia
dose:
- Pritchard (IM) regimen: loading 4 g IV (20% solution) over 5–10 min PLUS 10 g IM (5 g of 50% deep IM in each buttock with 1 mL 2% lidocaine); maintenance 5 g IM every 4 hours in alternate buttocks for 24 h after delivery or last seizure
- Zuspan (IV) regimen: 4 g IV over 5–10 min, then 1 g/hour infusion for 24 h
- Recurrent seizure: further 2 g IV over 5 min
- Acute asthma: 1.2–2 g IV over 20 minutes
ci:
- Myasthenia gravis; heart block; severe renal impairment (reduce doses)
se:
- Flushing, warmth, nausea, injection pain
- Toxicity: loss of patellar reflex (>7 mmol/L), respiratory depression (>10 mmol/L), cardiac arrest
ddi:
- Nifedipine :: Profound hypotension and neuromuscular blockade :: Use cautiously, monitor
- Neuromuscular blockers / aminoglycosides :: Prolonged blockade :: Caution
- Labetalol / other antihypertensives :: Hypotension :: Monitor
food:
- Not applicable
preg: Safe and life-saving in eclampsia.
lact: Compatible.
renal: Accumulates — if urine output <30 mL/h, withhold maintenance dose.
monitor:
- Before each dose: patellar reflexes present, respiratory rate ≥16/min, urine output ≥30 mL/hour (≥100 mL in 4 h)
- If respiratory arrest: ventilate and give calcium gluconate
advice:
- Explain the warm, flushing feeling on IV injection
storage: Below 25°C.
pearls:
- Antidote: calcium gluconate 10% 10 mL (1 g) IV over 10 minutes
- 50% solution must be diluted to 20% before IV injection

=== drug: ergometrine
name: Ergometrine
class: Ergot alkaloid uterotonic
group: Obstetric
nemlit: C (injection 0.5 mg/mL)
forms: Injection 0.5 mg/mL (also 0.2 mg); oxytocin/ergometrine combination
moa: α-adrenergic and serotonergic stimulation → sustained tetanic uterine contraction.
uses:
- Treatment of PPH due to uterine atony (second line after oxytocin)
dose:
- 0.2–0.5 mg IM or slow IV; may repeat after 15 min (max 1 mg)
ci:
- Hypertension, pre-eclampsia/eclampsia, heart disease, peripheral vascular disease
- First and second stages of labour; retained placenta (relative)
se:
- Hypertension, nausea, vomiting, headache, chest pain, vasospasm
ddi:
- Ritonavir / clarithromycin / azoles (CYP3A4 inhibitors) :: Ergotism (severe vasospasm) :: Contraindicated
food:
- Not applicable
preg: Postpartum only.
lact: Avoid repeated doses (suppresses lactation).
monitor:
- BP before and after; uterine tone
advice:
- Hospital use
storage: Cold chain 2–8°C, protect from light.
pearls:
- NEVER give ergometrine to a hypertensive or pre-eclamptic woman

=== drug: ferrous-sulfate
name: Ferrous sulfate (± folic acid)
class: Oral iron supplement
group: Haematology
nemlit: A (ferrous 200 mg sulfate or fumarate; oral liquid)
forms: Tablet 200 mg ferrous sulfate (65 mg elemental iron); ferrous sulfate + folic acid (antenatal); syrup
moa: Replaces iron for haemoglobin synthesis; absorbed as Fe2+ in duodenum.
uses:
- Treatment of iron deficiency anaemia; prevention in pregnancy (with folic acid)
- After deworming in hookworm anaemia; prophylaxis in low birth weight infants
dose:
- Treatment adults: 200 mg (65 mg elemental) two to three times daily; continue 3 months after Hb normalises
- Antenatal prevention: 60 mg elemental iron + 400 mcg folic acid daily
- Children: 3–6 mg/kg/day elemental iron in divided doses
ci:
- Haemochromatosis, haemosiderosis; anaemia not due to iron deficiency (e.g. thalassaemia) without iron deficiency; repeated transfusions
se:
- Nausea, epigastric pain, constipation or diarrhoea, black stools (harmless), teeth staining (liquids)
ddi:
- Ciprofloxacin / doxycycline / levothyroxine / dolutegravir / methyldopa :: Chelation → reduced absorption :: Separate by 2–4 hours
- Antacids / PPIs / calcium :: Reduced iron absorption :: Separate
- Zinc :: Mutual reduced absorption :: Separate
food:
- Tea, coffee, milk, calcium, eggs, cereals/bran :: Reduce absorption :: Avoid within 1–2 hours of the dose
- Vitamin C (orange juice, fruit) :: Enhances absorption :: Take with a vitamin C source
- Food :: Reduces GI upset but also absorption :: Empty stomach is best; take with food if not tolerated
preg: Recommended.
lact: Compatible.
monitor:
- Hb after 2–4 weeks (expect rise ~1 g/dL per 2–3 weeks); reticulocytes at 7–10 days; ferritin
advice:
- Stools will turn black — this is normal
- Take with orange juice or fruit, not with tea, coffee or milk
- Keep out of reach of children — iron overdose can kill a child
- Constipation: drink more water, eat vegetables and fruits
storage: Below 25°C in child-resistant containers.
pearls:
- Iron overdose antidote: deferoxamine

=== drug: folic-acid
name: Folic acid
class: Vitamin (B9)
group: Haematology
nemlit: A (tablet 5 mg; 400 mcg in antenatal combination)
forms: Tablet 5 mg; 400 mcg (with iron)
moa: Converted to tetrahydrofolate, needed for purine/thymidine synthesis and DNA replication.
uses:
- Prevention of neural tube defects (400 mcg daily pre-conception to 12 weeks; 5 mg if high risk: antiepileptics, previous NTD, diabetes, sickle cell)
- Megaloblastic anaemia due to folate deficiency; haemolytic states (sickle cell disease — lifelong), methotrexate rescue (folinic acid used for high dose)
dose:
- Folate deficiency: 5 mg daily for 4 months
- Sickle cell disease: 5 mg daily lifelong (some guidelines 1 mg)
- Pregnancy prevention: 400 mcg daily (5 mg if high risk)
ci:
- Undiagnosed megaloblastic anaemia (may mask B12 deficiency and allow neurological damage)
se:
- Rare (GI upset, allergic reactions)
ddi:
- Sulfadoxine-pyrimethamine :: High-dose (5 mg) folic acid antagonises SP efficacy :: Use 400 mcg in IPTp
- Phenytoin / phenobarbital :: Folic acid may lower anticonvulsant levels :: Monitor
- Methotrexate :: Antagonism (weekly folic acid used to reduce toxicity in RA) :: Give on a different day from methotrexate
food:
- None significant
preg: Recommended.
lact: Compatible.
monitor:
- Hb, MCV; check B12 before treating macrocytic anaemia
advice:
- Take daily; in sickle cell it must be taken every day for life
storage: Below 25°C.
pearls:
- Always exclude/treat B12 deficiency before giving folic acid alone for megaloblastic anaemia

=== drug: hydroxyurea
name: Hydroxyurea (hydroxycarbamide)
class: Antimetabolite (ribonucleotide reductase inhibitor); disease-modifying in sickle cell disease
group: Haematology
nemlit: S (capsule 500 mg)
forms: Capsule 500 mg; oral solution (paediatric)
moa: Inhibits ribonucleotide reductase; induces fetal haemoglobin (HbF) which inhibits HbS polymerisation; lowers neutrophils and adhesion.
uses:
- Sickle cell disease (≥9 months): reduces pain crises, acute chest syndrome, transfusion and mortality; stroke prevention
- CML and myeloproliferative disorders (specialist)
dose:
- SCD: start 15–20 mg/kg once daily, increase by 5 mg/kg every 8–12 weeks to maximum tolerated dose (≤35 mg/kg/day) keeping ANC >2 × 10⁹/L and platelets >80 × 10⁹/L
ci:
- Severe bone marrow suppression; pregnancy; breastfeeding
se:
- Myelosuppression (neutropenia, thrombocytopenia), macrocytosis (expected), GI upset, skin/nail hyperpigmentation, leg ulcers, reduced sperm count
ddi:
- Zidovudine / other myelosuppressives :: Additive marrow toxicity :: Monitor FBC
- Live vaccines :: Infection risk :: Avoid
food:
- None significant
preg: CONTRAINDICATED (teratogenic) — effective contraception for men and women.
lact: Avoid.
renal: Reduce dose if eGFR <60.
monitor:
- FBC every 2–4 weeks during titration, then every 2–3 months; HbF, renal and liver function
advice:
- Take at the same time daily; continue even when well
- Use reliable contraception; tell your clinician before planning pregnancy
- Caregivers: wear gloves when handling capsules; wash hands
- Report fever, unusual bleeding or bruising
storage: Below 25°C; cytotoxic — handle with care, keep away from children.
pearls:
- Rising MCV is a sign of adherence

=== drug: hydroxocobalamin
name: Hydroxocobalamin (vitamin B12)
class: Vitamin B12
group: Haematology
nemlit: C (injection 1 mg/mL)
forms: Injection 1 mg/mL
moa: Coenzyme for methionine synthase and methylmalonyl-CoA mutase — DNA synthesis and myelin maintenance.
uses:
- Pernicious anaemia and B12 deficiency (malabsorption, ileal disease, vegan diet, long-term metformin/PPI)
- Cyanide poisoning (high-dose IV form)
dose:
- Without neurological signs: 1 mg IM three times a week for 2 weeks, then 1 mg every 3 months
- With neurological signs: 1 mg IM on alternate days until no further improvement, then every 2 months
ci:
- Hypersensitivity
se:
- Injection-site pain, pruritus, hypokalaemia in early treatment of severe anaemia; pink urine
ddi:
- Chloramphenicol :: Reduced response :: Avoid
food:
- None
preg: Safe.
lact: Safe.
monitor:
- Hb, reticulocytes (day 5–7), K+ during initial treatment of severe anaemia
advice:
- Pernicious anaemia needs lifelong injections every 3 months
storage: Protect from light.
pearls:
- Oral B12 does not work when the problem is intrinsic factor deficiency

=== drug: vitamin-k
name: Phytomenadione (vitamin K1)
class: Vitamin K
group: Haematology
nemlit: B (injection 1 mg/0.5 mL, 2 mg/mL; neonatal 1 mg)
forms: Injection 1 mg/0.5 mL (neonatal), 10 mg/mL; oral
moa: Cofactor for γ-carboxylation of clotting factors II, VII, IX, X and proteins C, S.
uses:
- Prophylaxis of haemorrhagic disease of the newborn (all newborns)
- Reversal of warfarin over-anticoagulation; vitamin K deficiency bleeding (malabsorption, liver disease)
dose:
- Newborn: 1 mg IM at birth (0.5 mg if <1.5 kg)
- Warfarin: INR 5–8 no bleeding — withhold 1–2 doses; INR >8 — 1–5 mg orally; major bleeding — 5–10 mg slow IV with prothrombin complex/FFP
ci:
- Hypersensitivity
se:
- Anaphylactoid reactions with rapid IV injection; injection-site reactions
ddi:
- Warfarin :: Reverses effect (resistance for days after high doses) :: Use minimal effective dose
food:
- Not applicable
preg: Safe.
lact: Safe.
monitor:
- INR at 24 h
advice:
- Explain to new mothers why the baby gets the injection
storage: Protect from light.
pearls:
- IV vitamin K: give slowly (risk of anaphylaxis)

=== drug: tranexamic-acid
name: Tranexamic acid
class: Antifibrinolytic
group: Haematology
nemlit: C (injection 100 mg/mL; tablet 500 mg; syrup)
forms: Tablet 500 mg; injection 100 mg/mL (5 mL)
moa: Blocks lysine-binding sites on plasminogen → prevents fibrin degradation.
uses:
- PPH (within 3 hours of birth — WOMAN trial), trauma with major bleeding (CRASH-2, within 3 h)
- Menorrhagia, dental extraction in haemophilia, epistaxis, hereditary angioedema
dose:
- PPH/trauma: 1 g IV over 10 minutes; second 1 g if bleeding continues after 30 min (or recurs within 24 h)
- Menorrhagia: 1–1.5 g three times daily for up to 4 days from start of menses
ci:
- Active thromboembolic disease; history of venous/arterial thrombosis; haematuria (clot retention); severe renal impairment
se:
- Nausea, diarrhoea; rarely thrombosis, visual disturbance, seizures (high doses)
ddi:
- Combined oral contraceptives :: Increased thrombosis risk :: Caution
food:
- None
preg: Used for PPH.
lact: Compatible.
renal: Reduce dose.
monitor:
- Bleeding, signs of thrombosis
advice:
- Menorrhagia: take only during heavy bleeding days
storage: Below 25°C.
pearls:
- Must be given within 3 hours — benefit falls with each 15-minute delay

=== drug: iron-sucrose
name: Iron sucrose (IV iron)
class: Parenteral iron
group: Haematology
nemlit: D (injection 20 mg/mL)
forms: Injection 20 mg/mL (5 mL = 100 mg)
moa: Iron–sucrose complex taken up by the reticuloendothelial system, releasing iron to transferrin.
uses:
- Iron deficiency anaemia when oral iron fails, is not tolerated, or rapid repletion needed (late pregnancy, CKD on ESA)
dose:
- Total iron deficit (mg) = weight (kg) × (target Hb − actual Hb, g/dL) × 2.4 + iron stores (500 mg)
- Give 100–200 mg per session by slow IV injection/infusion (max 200 mg per dose, up to 3 times weekly)
ci:
- Non-iron-deficiency anaemia, iron overload, first trimester, history of hypersensitivity to parenteral iron
se:
- Hypotension, metallic taste, headache, nausea; hypersensitivity/anaphylaxis (rare)
ddi:
- Oral iron :: Reduced absorption :: Stop oral iron 5 days before IV
food:
- Not applicable
preg: Avoid in first trimester; used in 2nd/3rd.
lact: Compatible.
monitor:
- Observe 30 minutes after each dose for reactions; Hb after 2–4 weeks; ferritin
advice:
- Report itching, breathlessness or dizziness during the infusion
storage: Below 25°C; do not freeze.
pearls:
- Resuscitation facilities must be available when giving IV iron

=== drug: allopurinol
name: Allopurinol
class: Xanthine oxidase inhibitor (urate-lowering therapy)
group: Musculoskeletal
nemlit: B (tablet 100 mg, 300 mg)
forms: Tablet 100 mg, 300 mg
moa: Inhibits xanthine oxidase → ↓ conversion of hypoxanthine/xanthine to uric acid.
uses:
- Chronic gout with recurrent attacks, tophi, urate nephropathy/stones (urate-lowering therapy)
- Prevention of tumour lysis syndrome (start 1–2 days before chemotherapy)
dose:
- Gout: start 100 mg daily (50 mg in CKD), increase by 100 mg every 2–4 weeks to serum urate <0.36 mmol/L (6 mg/dL); usual 300 mg (max 900 mg)
- TLS prophylaxis: 300 mg daily (or 100 mg/m² three times daily)
ci:
- Acute gout attack (do not START during an attack — but do not stop if already taking)
- Previous severe hypersensitivity (HLA-B*5801 risk)
se:
- Rash (stop immediately — may progress to SJS/TEN/DRESS), acute gout flare on initiation
- GI upset, hepatotoxicity, hypersensitivity syndrome (higher in renal impairment)
ddi:
- Azathioprine / mercaptopurine :: Allopurinol blocks their metabolism → fatal marrow toxicity :: Reduce azathioprine to 25% or avoid
- Amoxicillin / ampicillin :: Increased rash :: Monitor
- Warfarin :: Raised INR :: Monitor
- Thiazides / loop diuretics :: Raise urate and hypersensitivity risk :: Review need for diuretic
food:
- Fluids :: Prevent urate stones :: Drink 2–3 L water daily
- Alcohol (especially beer), red meat, organ meats, seafood, sugary drinks :: Raise urate :: Limit
preg: Avoid.
lact: Compatible (low amounts).
renal: Start low (50–100 mg) and titrate slowly.
monitor:
- Serum urate every 2–4 weeks during titration; renal function, LFTs, rash
advice:
- Take after food with plenty of water, every day — it prevents attacks but does not treat an attack
- Attacks may increase in the first months — continue it and take colchicine/NSAID cover as prescribed
- Stop and seek advice at the first sign of a rash
storage: Below 25°C.
pearls:
- Start 2 weeks after an acute flare settles, with colchicine/NSAID prophylaxis for 3–6 months

=== drug: colchicine
name: Colchicine
class: Anti-gout (microtubule inhibitor)
group: Musculoskeletal
nemlit: Not found in NEMLIT 2021 extract (listed in STG gout chapter) — check facility list
forms: Tablet 0.5 mg (500 mcg)
moa: Binds tubulin → inhibits neutrophil migration and activation (NLRP3 inflammasome) at the joint.
uses:
- Acute gout flare (alternative to NSAIDs, e.g. in heart failure/anticoagulation)
- Prophylaxis when starting urate-lowering therapy; familial Mediterranean fever; pericarditis
dose:
- Acute gout: 500 mcg two to four times daily until relief (max 6 mg per course; not repeated within 3 days) — low-dose regimen 1 mg then 0.5 mg after 1 hour
- Prophylaxis: 500 mcg once or twice daily
ci:
- Severe renal or hepatic impairment; blood dyscrasias; with strong CYP3A4/P-gp inhibitors in renal/hepatic impairment
se:
- Diarrhoea, nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain (dose-limiting — first sign of toxicity)
- Myopathy, neuropathy, bone marrow suppression in overdose
ddi:
- Clarithromycin / erythromycin / ritonavir / ketoconazole :: Colchicine toxicity (fatal) :: Avoid or greatly reduce dose
- Statins :: Myopathy :: Monitor
food:
- Grapefruit juice :: Raises levels :: Avoid
preg: Avoid.
lact: Compatible.
renal: Reduce dose; avoid if eGFR <10.
monitor:
- GI symptoms
advice:
- Start at the first sign of an attack; stop if diarrhoea or vomiting develop
storage: Below 25°C.
pearls:
- Very narrow therapeutic index — never exceed maximum course dose

=== drug: tamsulosin
name: Tamsulosin
class: Selective alpha-1A adrenoceptor blocker
group: Urology
nemlit: D (tablet/capsule 0.4 mg)
forms: Modified-release capsule 0.4 mg
moa: Relaxes prostatic and bladder neck smooth muscle (α1A) → improved urine flow.
uses:
- Lower urinary tract symptoms of benign prostatic hyperplasia
- Medical expulsive therapy for distal ureteric stones
dose:
- 0.4 mg once daily after the same meal each day
ci:
- History of orthostatic hypotension; severe hepatic impairment
se:
- Dizziness, postural hypotension (first dose), abnormal/retrograde ejaculation, nasal congestion
- Intra-operative floppy iris syndrome (cataract surgery)
ddi:
- Other antihypertensives / PDE-5 inhibitors (sildenafil) :: Additive hypotension :: Separate dosing, start low
- Strong CYP3A4 inhibitors :: Raised levels :: Caution
food:
- Food :: Consistent absorption :: Take 30 minutes after the same meal daily
preg: Not applicable.
lact: Not applicable.
monitor:
- Symptom scores (IPSS), BP
advice:
- Swallow whole; first dose at bedtime; rise slowly
- Tell your eye surgeon before cataract surgery
storage: Below 25°C.
pearls:
- Alpha-blockers relieve symptoms within days; 5-ARIs shrink the prostate over 6 months

=== drug: finasteride
name: Finasteride
class: 5-alpha reductase inhibitor
group: Urology
nemlit: D (tablet 5 mg)
forms: Tablet 5 mg (1 mg for alopecia)
moa: Inhibits type II 5α-reductase → ↓ DHT → prostate shrinkage (~20–30% over 6 months).
uses:
- BPH with enlarged prostate (alone or with tamsulosin)
dose:
- 5 mg once daily (benefit after 3–6 months)
ci:
- Women and children; women who are or may become pregnant must not handle crushed/broken tablets
se:
- Reduced libido, erectile dysfunction, ejaculatory disorder, gynaecomastia; reduces PSA by ~50%
ddi:
- Few significant interactions
food:
- None
preg: Contraindicated (feminisation of male fetus).
lact: Not applicable.
monitor:
- Symptoms; double the measured PSA when interpreting
advice:
- Takes months to work; pregnant women should not touch broken tablets; use condoms if partner is pregnant
storage: Below 25°C.
pearls:
- PSA halves on finasteride — multiply by 2 for cancer screening

=== drug: sildenafil
name: Sildenafil
class: Phosphodiesterase-5 (PDE-5) inhibitor
group: Urology
nemlit: S (tablet 50 mg)
forms: Tablet 25 mg, 50 mg, 100 mg
moa: Inhibits PDE-5 → ↑ cGMP in corpus cavernosum (requires sexual stimulation) and pulmonary vasculature.
uses:
- Erectile dysfunction; pulmonary arterial hypertension (specialist)
dose:
- ED: 50 mg about 1 hour before sexual activity (25–100 mg), max once daily
ci:
- Nitrates (GTN, isosorbide) — life-threatening hypotension; recent stroke/MI, severe hypotension, unstable angina
se:
- Headache, flushing, dyspepsia, nasal congestion, visual disturbance (blue tinge), hypotension; priapism (rare); NAION
ddi:
- Nitrates (GTN, isosorbide dinitrate) :: Severe hypotension, death :: Absolutely contraindicated (24 h gap)
- Alpha-blockers (tamsulosin, doxazosin) :: Hypotension :: Start low, separate
- Ritonavir / clarithromycin :: Greatly increased levels :: Max 25 mg every 48 h
food:
- High-fat meal :: Delays onset :: Take on an empty stomach for faster action
- Grapefruit :: Raises levels :: Avoid
- Alcohol :: Hypotension :: Limit
preg: Not applicable.
lact: Not applicable.
monitor:
- Cardiovascular fitness for sexual activity
advice:
- Seek emergency help for an erection lasting more than 4 hours
- Never use with chest-pain sprays or tablets (nitrates)
storage: Below 25°C.
pearls:
- ED may be the first sign of cardiovascular disease or diabetes — screen

=== drug: adrenaline
name: Adrenaline (epinephrine)
class: Sympathomimetic (α and β agonist)
group: Emergency
nemlit: A (injection 1 mg/mL [1:1000] in 1 mL ampoule)
forms: Injection 1 mg/mL (1:1000); 1:10,000 (0.1 mg/mL) for IV in arrest; auto-injector 0.3 mg/0.15 mg
moa: α1 vasoconstriction (raises BP, reduces oedema), β1 inotropy/chronotropy, β2 bronchodilation and mast-cell stabilisation.
uses:
- Anaphylaxis (first-line, life-saving)
- Cardiac arrest (all rhythms); severe croup (nebulised); refractory hypotension/septic shock (infusion)
dose:
- Anaphylaxis: 0.5 mg (0.5 mL of 1:1000) IM into anterolateral thigh (adults); children 0.01 mg/kg (max 0.5 mg) — 6–12 years 0.3 mg; <6 years 0.15 mg; repeat every 5 minutes if needed
- Cardiac arrest: 1 mg IV (10 mL of 1:10,000) every 3–5 minutes
- Croup: 0.5 mL/kg of 1:1000 (max 5 mL) nebulised
ci:
- None in anaphylaxis/arrest (life-threatening situations)
se:
- Tachycardia, arrhythmias, hypertension, anxiety, tremor, headache, pallor
- Tissue necrosis with extravasation; never inject into fingers/toes (1:1000)
ddi:
- Non-selective beta-blockers (propranolol) :: Unopposed α effect → severe hypertension, reduced response in anaphylaxis :: Glucagon if refractory
- TCAs / MAOIs :: Hypertension, arrhythmias :: Caution
- Halothane :: Arrhythmias :: Caution
food:
- Not applicable
preg: Use in anaphylaxis/arrest without hesitation.
lact: Compatible.
monitor:
- BP, HR, ECG, airway, SpO2
advice:
- Patients at risk: carry an adrenaline auto-injector; inject into the outer thigh through clothing and call for help
storage: Protect from light (discard if pink/brown); below 25°C.
pearls:
- 1:1000 = 1 mg/mL (IM); 1:10,000 = 0.1 mg/mL (IV) — confusing them is a classic fatal error
- IM thigh is preferred over SC in anaphylaxis (faster absorption)

=== drug: dextrose-50
name: Dextrose (glucose) 50% / 10%
class: Carbohydrate / antihypoglycaemic
group: Emergency
nemlit: Listed under solutions (glucose 5%, 10%, 50%)
forms: Injection 50% (50 mL), 10% (500 mL), 5% infusion
moa: Rapidly raises blood glucose.
uses:
- Hypoglycaemia (unconscious or unable to swallow); hyperkalaemia (with insulin); DKA (add 5% dextrose when glucose ≈ 11–14 mmol/L)
- Severe malaria hypoglycaemia; neonatal hypoglycaemia (10%)
dose:
- Adult hypoglycaemia: 25–50 mL of 50% IV (or 100–200 mL of 10%) — then recheck in 15 minutes
- Children: 10% dextrose 2–5 mL/kg IV (never 50% in children — dilute)
- Hyperkalaemia: 50 mL 50% with 10 units soluble insulin
ci:
- Hyperglycaemia; 50% via small peripheral vein in children (phlebitis, necrosis)
se:
- Phlebitis, tissue necrosis if extravasated (hypertonic), hyperglycaemia, hypokalaemia
ddi:
- Insulin :: Used together for hyperkalaemia :: Monitor glucose and K+
food:
- Follow with oral complex carbohydrate when conscious
