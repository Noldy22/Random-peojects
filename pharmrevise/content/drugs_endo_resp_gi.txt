# Drug monographs — endocrine, respiratory, gastrointestinal.

=== drug: metformin
name: Metformin
class: Biguanide (oral antidiabetic)
group: Endocrine
nemlit: A (tablet 500, 750, 850, 1000 mg; SR)
forms: Tablet 500 mg, 850 mg, 1000 mg; modified-release 500 mg, 750 mg
moa: Decreases hepatic gluconeogenesis, improves peripheral insulin sensitivity, slightly reduces intestinal glucose absorption; activates AMP-kinase. Does not stimulate insulin release.
uses:
- First-line drug for type 2 diabetes (with lifestyle change), especially if overweight
- Pre-diabetes in high-risk patients; polycystic ovary syndrome (off-label)
dose:
- 500 mg once or twice daily with meals, increase by 500 mg every 1–2 weeks; usual 1–2 g/day; max 2–3 g/day in divided doses
- eGFR 30–45: max 1 g/day; stop if eGFR <30
ci:
- eGFR <30 mL/min; acute conditions causing tissue hypoxia (shock, sepsis, acute heart failure, respiratory failure)
- Severe hepatic impairment, alcohol abuse; diabetic ketoacidosis
- Iodinated contrast or general anaesthesia (withhold on the day and 48 h after)
se:
- GI upset: nausea, diarrhoea, abdominal discomfort, metallic taste (common at start — reduce by slow titration and taking with food)
- Vitamin B12 deficiency with long-term use
- Lactic acidosis (rare but serious)
ddi:
- Iodinated contrast media :: AKI → metformin accumulation → lactic acidosis :: Withhold before and 48 h after; recheck creatinine
- Dolutegravir :: Raises metformin levels ~2-fold :: Limit metformin to 1 g/day when starting DTG; monitor glucose
- Alcohol :: Lactic acidosis and hypoglycaemia risk :: Avoid heavy drinking
- Corticosteroids / thiazides :: Raise blood glucose :: Monitor glucose
- Cimetidine :: Raises metformin levels :: Monitor
food:
- Food :: Reduces GI side effects :: Take with or just after meals
- Alcohol :: Lactic acidosis :: Avoid binge drinking
preg: Used in pregnancy in some guidelines; STG prefers insulin for gestational diabetes.
lact: Compatible.
renal: Reduce if eGFR 30–45; stop if <30.
hepatic: Avoid in significant hepatic impairment.
monitor:
- HbA1c every 3 months until controlled, then 6-monthly; fasting glucose
- Renal function at least annually (more often if eGFR <60); vitamin B12 if neuropathy or anaemia
advice:
- Take with meals; stomach upset usually settles in 1–2 weeks
- Metformin alone does not cause hypoglycaemia
- Sick-day rule: stop temporarily if vomiting, diarrhoea or dehydrated, and restart when eating normally
- Tell staff before any X-ray dye or operation
storage: Below 25°C.
pearls:
- Weight-neutral or modest weight loss — preferred in obese type 2 diabetes
- "Sick day rules" for metformin: SADMANS drugs (Sulfonylureas, ACEi, Diuretics, Metformin, ARBs, NSAIDs, SGLT2i)

=== drug: glibenclamide
name: Glibenclamide
class: Sulfonylurea (second generation, long-acting)
group: Endocrine
nemlit: A (tablet 5 mg)
forms: Tablet 5 mg
moa: Binds the sulfonylurea receptor on pancreatic β-cell K-ATP channels → depolarisation → insulin release. Needs functioning β-cells.
uses:
- Type 2 diabetes (add-on to metformin or when metformin is contraindicated)
dose:
- 2.5–5 mg once daily with breakfast; increase by 2.5 mg weekly; max 15 mg/day (doses >10 mg divided)
ci:
- Type 1 diabetes, DKA; severe renal or hepatic impairment
- Elderly (prolonged hypoglycaemia) — prefer gliclazide; pregnancy (insulin preferred); sulfonamide allergy; G6PD deficiency (haemolysis)
se:
- Hypoglycaemia (can be prolonged and severe, especially in elderly and renal impairment)
- Weight gain; GI upset; rash, photosensitivity; rarely cholestatic jaundice, blood dyscrasias
ddi:
- Co-trimoxazole / fluconazole / clarithromycin :: Increased sulfonylurea levels → hypoglycaemia :: Monitor glucose; reduce dose
- Beta-blockers :: Mask hypoglycaemia warning signs :: Educate patient
- Alcohol :: Hypoglycaemia; disulfiram-like flushing :: Avoid
- Rifampicin :: Reduced effect (enzyme induction) :: Monitor glucose
- Insulin :: Additive hypoglycaemia :: Reduce or stop sulfonylurea when insulin started
- Corticosteroids / thiazides :: Reduced hypoglycaemic effect :: Monitor
food:
- Missing or delaying meals :: Hypoglycaemia :: Eat regular meals; never skip after taking the dose
- Alcohol :: Hypoglycaemia :: Avoid
preg: Avoid — switch to insulin.
lact: Avoid (neonatal hypoglycaemia).
renal: Avoid in renal impairment (active metabolites) — gliclazide preferred.
hepatic: Avoid in severe impairment.
monitor:
- Blood glucose, HbA1c; hypoglycaemia episodes; weight
advice:
- Take with breakfast; never skip meals
- Learn the signs of low sugar (sweating, shaking, hunger, confusion) — take 3 sugar cubes/sweet drink, then a snack
- Carry sugar or sweets at all times; tell family how to help
- Ramadan/fasting: discuss dose timing with your pharmacist
storage: Below 25°C.
pearls:
- Longest-acting common sulfonylurea — highest hypoglycaemia risk ("Beers list" for elderly)

=== drug: gliclazide
name: Gliclazide
class: Sulfonylurea (second generation, shorter-acting)
group: Endocrine
nemlit: A (tablet 40, 80 mg; MR 30, 60 mg)
forms: Tablet 40 mg, 80 mg; modified-release 30 mg, 60 mg
moa: Stimulates insulin release from β-cells (K-ATP channel closure); metabolised by liver to inactive metabolites.
uses:
- Type 2 diabetes, preferred sulfonylurea in elderly and mild–moderate renal impairment
dose:
- 40–80 mg daily with breakfast, increased to 160 mg; doses >160 mg divided (max 320 mg/day)
- Modified-release: 30 mg daily with breakfast, increase every 4 weeks to max 120 mg
ci:
- Type 1 diabetes, DKA; severe renal or hepatic impairment; pregnancy; G6PD deficiency (caution)
se:
- Hypoglycaemia (less than glibenclamide), weight gain, GI upset, rash
ddi:
- Fluconazole / co-trimoxazole / clarithromycin :: Hypoglycaemia :: Monitor
- Beta-blockers :: Masked hypoglycaemia :: Educate
- Alcohol :: Hypoglycaemia :: Avoid
- Rifampicin :: Reduced effect :: Monitor
food:
- Skipping meals :: Hypoglycaemia :: Take with breakfast, eat regularly
preg: Avoid — use insulin.
lact: Avoid.
renal: Use with caution and lower doses; avoid if severe.
monitor:
- Glucose, HbA1c, hypoglycaemia episodes
advice:
- Take with breakfast; swallow MR tablets whole
- Same hypoglycaemia counselling as for all sulfonylureas
storage: Below 25°C.
pearls:
- Lower hypoglycaemia risk than glibenclamide — prefer in elderly

=== drug: insulin-soluble
name: Insulin — soluble (regular, short-acting)
class: Short-acting human insulin
group: Endocrine
nemlit: A (injection 40 IU/mL, 100 IU/mL)
forms: Vial 100 IU/mL (also 40 IU/mL — match syringe to strength!); pens
moa: Replaces endogenous insulin: ↑ glucose uptake into muscle/fat, ↓ hepatic glucose output, ↓ lipolysis and ketogenesis; drives K+ into cells.
uses:
- Type 1 diabetes (bolus with meals in basal-bolus regimen)
- Type 2 diabetes when oral agents fail; gestational diabetes
- DKA and HHS (IV infusion); hyperkalaemia (10 units IV with 50 mL of 50% dextrose); perioperative glucose control
dose:
- Basal-bolus: total daily dose 0.5–1 unit/kg/day — ~50% as basal (NPH), ~50% as soluble split before meals
- DKA: 0.1 unit/kg/hour IV infusion (after fluids started and K+ ≥3.3 mmol/L)
- Hyperkalaemia: 10 units soluble insulin IV in 50 mL 50% dextrose over 15–30 min
ci:
- Hypoglycaemia
se:
- Hypoglycaemia (most important)
- Weight gain; lipohypertrophy at repeated injection sites; hypokalaemia (IV); rarely allergy
ddi:
- Beta-blockers :: Mask hypoglycaemia; prolong recovery :: Monitor glucose
- Alcohol :: Hypoglycaemia :: Limit; never drink on an empty stomach
- Corticosteroids / thiazides / protease inhibitors :: Raise glucose → higher insulin need :: Adjust dose
- Sulfonylureas :: Additive hypoglycaemia :: Reduce/stop sulfonylurea
food:
- Meals :: Soluble insulin must match a meal :: Inject 30 minutes before eating; do not skip the meal
- Alcohol :: Delayed hypoglycaemia :: Eat when drinking; check glucose at bedtime
preg: Insulin is the drug of choice in pregnancy; requirements rise in 2nd/3rd trimesters and fall immediately after delivery.
lact: Compatible.
renal: Requirements fall in renal impairment (reduced clearance).
monitor:
- Self-monitored blood glucose (before meals and bedtime), HbA1c 3-monthly
- K+ hourly-2 hourly during IV insulin in DKA
advice:
- Inject 30 minutes before meals into abdomen/thigh/upper arm; rotate sites within the same area
- Check the strength on the vial matches the syringe (U-100 with U-100 syringe)
- Hypoglycaemia: take 15–20 g fast sugar, recheck in 15 min, then a snack
- Sick days: never stop insulin — check glucose more often and seek help if vomiting
- Store unopened vials in the fridge (2–8°C), never freeze; vial in use can be kept below 25–30°C for 28 days (use a clay pot/cool place if no fridge)
storage: Unopened 2–8°C (do not freeze); in-use vial at room temperature up to 28 days, away from sunlight.
pearls:
- Only soluble (regular) insulin can be given IV
- When mixing soluble + NPH in one syringe: draw up clear (soluble) before cloudy (NPH)

=== drug: insulin-nph
name: Insulin — isophane (NPH) and premixed 30/70
class: Intermediate-acting human insulin; premixed biphasic insulin
group: Endocrine
nemlit: A (intermediate/isophane); premixed insulin S
forms: Vial 100 IU/mL (cloudy suspension); premixed 30% soluble/70% NPH
moa: Protamine-complexed insulin with delayed absorption (onset 1–2 h, peak 4–12 h, duration 12–18 h); provides basal cover.
uses:
- Basal insulin in type 1 and type 2 diabetes (bedtime NPH added to oral agents)
- Twice-daily premixed regimen (common in resource-limited settings)
dose:
- Insulin-naïve type 2: 0.2 units/kg (or 10 units) at bedtime, titrate by 2–4 units every 3 days to fasting glucose 4–7 mmol/L
- Premixed: total dose 0.4–0.6 units/kg/day, 2/3 before breakfast and 1/3 before evening meal
ci:
- Hypoglycaemia; never IV
se:
- Hypoglycaemia (especially nocturnal 2–4 am), weight gain, lipohypertrophy
ddi:
- Beta-blockers :: Masked hypoglycaemia :: Monitor
- Alcohol :: Hypoglycaemia :: Limit
- Corticosteroids :: Higher insulin need :: Adjust
food:
- Meals :: Premixed insulin needs regular meals 30 min after injection :: Do not skip meals
preg: Safe; preferred.
lact: Compatible.
monitor:
- Fasting glucose (titrate basal dose), pre-dinner glucose for premixed, HbA1c
advice:
- Roll the vial gently 10–20 times between your palms to mix (do not shake)
- Inject the same time each day; rotate sites
- Watch for night-time lows: nightmares, sweating, morning headache — have a bedtime snack if needed
storage: As for soluble insulin (2–8°C unopened; 28 days in use).
pearls:
- Cloudy insulins must be resuspended before each dose

=== drug: levothyroxine
name: Levothyroxine
class: Thyroid hormone (synthetic T4)
group: Endocrine
nemlit: D (tablet 50 mcg)
forms: Tablet 25 mcg, 50 mcg, 100 mcg
moa: Synthetic T4, converted peripherally to active T3; restores euthyroid state.
uses:
- Hypothyroidism (primary, post-thyroidectomy, post-radioiodine); congenital hypothyroidism
- TSH suppression after thyroid cancer (specialist)
dose:
- Adults <60 years without heart disease: 1.6 mcg/kg/day (typically 50–100 mcg daily), adjust by 25 mcg every 4–6 weeks
- Elderly or ischaemic heart disease: start 12.5–25 mcg daily, increase slowly
ci:
- Untreated adrenal insufficiency (correct cortisol first); thyrotoxicosis; acute MI
se:
- Over-replacement: palpitations, tremor, insomnia, weight loss, heat intolerance, AF, osteoporosis
- Angina in ischaemic heart disease
ddi:
- Ferrous sulfate / calcium / antacids / PPIs :: Reduce absorption :: Separate by 4 hours
- Rifampicin / carbamazepine / phenytoin :: Increase metabolism → higher dose needed :: Monitor TSH
- Warfarin :: Increased anticoagulant effect when becoming euthyroid :: Monitor INR
- Insulin / oral antidiabetics :: Requirement may rise :: Monitor glucose
food:
- Food, coffee, soya, high-fibre :: Reduce absorption :: Take on an empty stomach 30–60 min before breakfast with water
preg: Essential — requirements rise 25–50% in pregnancy; increase dose as soon as pregnancy confirmed and check TSH each trimester.
lact: Compatible.
monitor:
- TSH 6–8 weeks after each dose change; once stable, every 6–12 months
advice:
- Take every morning on an empty stomach, 30–60 minutes before breakfast or tea/coffee
- Keep iron or calcium tablets at least 4 hours apart
- Lifelong treatment; symptoms improve over weeks
- Report palpitations, chest pain, tremor
storage: Below 25°C, protect from light and moisture.
pearls:
- Long half-life (7 days) — dose changes take 6 weeks to show in TSH

=== drug: carbimazole
name: Carbimazole
class: Thionamide antithyroid drug
group: Endocrine
nemlit: C (tablet 5 mg); propylthiouracil D (tablet 50 mg)
forms: Tablet 5 mg, 20 mg
moa: Pro-drug of methimazole; inhibits thyroid peroxidase → blocks iodination and coupling, reducing T4/T3 synthesis.
uses:
- Hyperthyroidism (Graves' disease, toxic multinodular goitre) — before surgery/radioiodine or for 12–18-month course
dose:
- Initial 15–40 mg daily (single or divided), reduce to 5–15 mg daily maintenance once euthyroid (4–8 weeks); or block-and-replace
ci:
- Severe blood disorders; previous agranulocytosis with thionamides
- First trimester of pregnancy (use propylthiouracil)
se:
- AGRANULOCYTOSIS (rare, serious): sore throat, fever, mouth ulcers
- Rash, pruritus (common), arthralgia, nausea, taste disturbance, hepatotoxicity (rare)
ddi:
- Warfarin :: Effect changes as thyroid status changes :: Monitor INR
- Digoxin :: Levels rise as patient becomes euthyroid :: Monitor
food:
- Iodine-rich foods / supplements :: May reduce effectiveness :: Avoid excess iodine
preg: Avoid in 1st trimester (aplasia cutis, embryopathy) — use PTU, then switch to carbimazole for 2nd/3rd trimester.
lact: Compatible at low doses.
monitor:
- TFTs every 4–6 weeks; FBC if sore throat or fever
advice:
- STOP and seek a blood test urgently if you get a sore throat, mouth ulcers, fever or flu-like illness
- Propranolol may be given for palpitations initially
- Women: tell your clinician before trying to conceive
storage: Below 25°C.
pearls:
- PTU is preferred in thyroid storm and 1st trimester; it causes more hepatotoxicity

=== drug: prednisolone
name: Prednisolone
class: Systemic corticosteroid (glucocorticoid)
group: Endocrine
nemlit: A (tablet 5 mg)
forms: Tablet 5 mg (also 1 mg, 25 mg); soluble tablet; eye drops 0.5–1%
moa: Binds intracellular glucocorticoid receptors → suppresses inflammatory gene transcription (↓ prostaglandins, leukotrienes, cytokines), immunosuppression.
uses:
- Acute asthma and COPD exacerbations; severe allergic reactions (after adrenaline)
- Inflammatory and autoimmune disease (RA, SLE, nephrotic syndrome, IBD, autoimmune haemolytic anaemia, ITP)
- Haematological malignancy regimens (CHOP, ALL induction); TB IRIS, TB meningitis/pericarditis; PCP (adjunct)
dose:
- Acute asthma: adults 40–50 mg once daily for 5–7 days (children 1–2 mg/kg, max 40 mg) — no taper needed for short courses
- ITP/AIHA: 1 mg/kg/day then taper
- Rheumatoid arthritis bridging: 5–10 mg daily
ci:
- Untreated systemic infection (relative), live vaccines with immunosuppressive doses
se:
- Short term: hyperglycaemia, insomnia, mood change/psychosis, increased appetite, dyspepsia, fluid retention, hypertension
- Long term: Cushingoid features, osteoporosis, adrenal suppression, infection risk, cataracts, glaucoma, myopathy, skin thinning, growth suppression in children
ddi:
- NSAIDs / aspirin :: Peptic ulceration and GI bleeding :: Add PPI
- Rifampicin / phenytoin / carbamazepine :: Increased metabolism → reduced effect :: Double steroid dose with rifampicin
- Antidiabetic drugs / insulin :: Hyperglycaemia :: Monitor glucose, increase therapy
- Diuretics / salbutamol :: Hypokalaemia :: Monitor K+
- Live vaccines :: Risk of vaccine infection :: Avoid with high doses
food:
- Food :: Reduces gastric irritation :: Take in the morning with food
- Salt :: Fluid retention :: Moderate salt intake
- Alcohol :: Peptic ulcer risk :: Limit
preg: Prednisolone is largely inactivated by the placenta — preferred steroid in pregnancy when needed.
lact: Compatible.
monitor:
- BP, glucose, weight; bone health and eyes with long-term use
advice:
- Take as a single dose in the morning with food
- Do not stop suddenly after more than 3 weeks of treatment — dose must be tapered
- Carry a steroid card if on long-term treatment; avoid contact with chickenpox/measles
- Report black stools, severe mood changes, signs of infection
storage: Below 25°C.
pearls:
- 5 mg prednisolone ≈ 20 mg hydrocortisone ≈ 0.75 mg dexamethasone
- Courses <3 weeks generally need no taper (unless repeated courses)

=== drug: hydrocortisone
name: Hydrocortisone
class: Corticosteroid (glucocorticoid with mineralocorticoid activity)
group: Endocrine
nemlit: A (injection 100 mg; cream 0.5–1%)
forms: Powder for injection 100 mg (as sodium succinate); tablet 10 mg, 20 mg; cream 1%
moa: Glucocorticoid and mineralocorticoid receptor agonist — physiological cortisol replacement.
uses:
- Anaphylaxis and severe asthma (adjunct), adrenal crisis, septic shock refractory to fluids and vasopressors
- Adrenal insufficiency replacement (oral); thyroid storm; topical eczema/dermatitis
dose:
- Anaphylaxis/acute severe asthma: 100–200 mg IV (children 4 mg/kg)
- Adrenal crisis: 100 mg IV stat, then 50–100 mg every 6–8 hours
- Septic shock: 50 mg IV every 6 hours
- Replacement: 15–25 mg/day orally in 2–3 doses (largest in the morning)
ci:
- Systemic fungal infection (relative); topical: untreated skin infection, rosacea
se:
- As for prednisolone; plus sodium and fluid retention, hypokalaemia (mineralocorticoid effect)
ddi:
- Rifampicin / phenytoin :: Reduced effect :: Increase dose
- NSAIDs :: GI ulceration :: Gastroprotection
- Insulin / antidiabetics :: Hyperglycaemia :: Monitor
food:
- Food :: Less GI irritation with oral form :: Take with food
preg: Safe when needed.
lact: Compatible.
monitor:
- BP, glucose, electrolytes
advice:
- Adrenal insufficiency: double oral dose when febrile/ill ("sick day rules"); carry a steroid card
- Topical: apply thinly, short courses on the face
storage: Below 25°C.
pearls:
- Antihistamines and steroids are ADJUNCTS in anaphylaxis — adrenaline IM is first-line

=== drug: dexamethasone
name: Dexamethasone
class: Long-acting potent glucocorticoid
group: Endocrine
nemlit: B (injection 4 mg/mL); D (tablet 0.5 mg, 4 mg)
forms: Injection 4 mg/mL; tablet 0.5 mg, 4 mg; eye drops
moa: Potent glucocorticoid with negligible mineralocorticoid action; long half-life.
uses:
- Fetal lung maturation in threatened preterm birth (<34 weeks)
- Cerebral oedema (tumour), malignant spinal cord compression, SVC obstruction
- Bacterial meningitis adjunct (before/with first antibiotic dose); severe COVID-19 (hypoxic)
- Antiemetic with chemotherapy; croup; allergic reactions
dose:
- Fetal lung maturity: 6 mg IM every 12 hours × 4 doses
- Spinal cord compression: 16 mg/day (e.g. 8 mg twice daily) with PPI, then taper
- Meningitis: 0.15 mg/kg IV every 6 hours for 2–4 days
- Croup: 0.15–0.6 mg/kg orally once
ci:
- Systemic fungal infection; cerebral malaria (not beneficial)
se:
- Hyperglycaemia, insomnia, psychiatric effects, GI ulceration, immunosuppression, perineal burning if IV bolus fast
ddi:
- Rifampicin / phenytoin / carbamazepine :: Reduced effect :: Increase dose
- Antidiabetics :: Hyperglycaemia :: Monitor
- Dolutegravir / rilpivirine :: Dexamethasone (repeated) lowers their levels :: Avoid long courses or monitor VL
food:
- Food :: Reduce GI upset :: Take with food
preg: Used deliberately for fetal lung maturation.
lact: Compatible for short courses.
monitor:
- Glucose (especially in diabetics), BP, mental state
advice:
- Take in the morning to reduce sleeplessness
storage: Protect injection from light.
pearls:
- Steroids are NOT recommended in cerebral malaria (STG)

=== drug: salbutamol
name: Salbutamol
class: Short-acting beta-2 agonist (SABA) bronchodilator
group: Respiratory
nemlit: A (inhaler 100 mcg/dose, nebuliser solution, tablet 4 mg, syrup 2 mg/5 mL)
forms: Metered-dose inhaler 100 mcg/puff; nebuliser solution 5 mg/mL (2.5 mg, 5 mg nebules); tablet 2 mg, 4 mg; syrup 2 mg/5 mL
moa: Stimulates β2 receptors on bronchial smooth muscle → ↑ cAMP → bronchodilation within minutes (lasts 4–6 h); also shifts K+ into cells.
uses:
- Relief of acute bronchospasm in asthma and COPD ("reliever")
- Acute severe asthma (nebulised, repeated); exercise-induced asthma prevention
- Hyperkalaemia (adjunct, nebulised 10–20 mg)
dose:
- MDI: 100–200 mcg (1–2 puffs) as required, up to 4 times daily; with spacer in children
- Acute asthma: 2.5–5 mg nebulised every 20 minutes for the first hour (or 4–10 puffs via spacer), then 1–4 hourly
- Oral (less preferred): adults 2–4 mg 3–4 times daily
ci:
- Hypersensitivity; caution in hyperthyroidism, arrhythmias, ischaemic heart disease
se:
- Fine tremor, tachycardia, palpitations, headache, muscle cramps
- Hypokalaemia (high doses), lactic acidosis with continuous nebulisation
ddi:
- Non-selective beta-blockers (propranolol) :: Block bronchodilator effect; can precipitate bronchospasm :: Avoid β-blockers in asthma
- Diuretics / steroids / theophylline :: Additive hypokalaemia :: Monitor K+ in severe asthma
food:
- Caffeine (large amounts) :: Additive tremor and palpitations :: Moderate intake
preg: Safe — uncontrolled asthma is a greater risk to the baby.
lact: Compatible.
monitor:
- Peak flow, symptom frequency, reliever use (>2 times/week = poor control)
advice:
- Inhaler technique: shake, breathe out, seal lips, press and breathe in slowly and deeply, hold 10 seconds; wait 30–60 s before a second puff
- Use a spacer (or improvised plastic-bottle spacer) for children and during attacks
- If you need your reliever more than twice a week, see your clinician — you need a preventer
- Seek urgent care if the inhaler is not lasting 4 hours or you cannot speak in sentences
storage: Below 30°C; do not puncture or burn canister; clean actuator weekly.
pearls:
- SABA-only treatment is no longer recommended — every asthmatic should have an inhaled corticosteroid-containing treatment (GINA)

=== drug: beclometasone
name: Beclometasone dipropionate (inhaled)
class: Inhaled corticosteroid (ICS) — "preventer"
group: Respiratory
nemlit: Inhaled steroid listed as budesonide inhaler B (100, 200 mcg); beclometasone per STG asthma chapter
forms: Metered-dose inhaler 50 mcg, 100 mcg, 250 mcg per puff
moa: Local anti-inflammatory action in the airway: ↓ eosinophils, mast cells, mucosal oedema and airway hyper-responsiveness.
uses:
- Persistent asthma (step 2 onwards) — maintenance controller
- COPD with frequent exacerbations (with long-acting bronchodilator)
dose:
- Adults: 100–400 mcg twice daily (low dose 200–500 mcg/day; medium 500–1000; high >1000)
- Children: 50–200 mcg twice daily via spacer
ci:
- Not for relief of acute attacks; untreated pulmonary TB (caution)
se:
- Oral candidiasis (thrush), hoarseness (dysphonia), sore throat
- High doses: adrenal suppression, growth slowing in children, cataracts, osteoporosis, pneumonia in COPD
ddi:
- Ritonavir (boosted PIs) :: Raises systemic steroid levels (more with fluticasone/budesonide) → Cushing's :: Prefer beclometasone; monitor
food:
- None significant
preg: Safe — continue throughout pregnancy.
lact: Compatible.
monitor:
- Asthma control, inhaler technique, growth in children
advice:
- Use every day even when well — it prevents attacks; benefit builds over 1–2 weeks
- Rinse mouth with water and spit out after each use (prevents thrush and hoarseness)
- Use a spacer
storage: Below 30°C; protect from frost and direct sunlight.
pearls:
- Rinse-and-spit after ICS use is a classic counselling point

=== drug: budesonide-formoterol
name: Budesonide / formoterol (inhaler)
class: Inhaled corticosteroid + long-acting beta-2 agonist (ICS-LABA)
group: Respiratory
nemlit: Budesonide inhaler B (100, 200 mcg); combination per facility availability
forms: Dry-powder inhaler 160/4.5 mcg, 80/4.5 mcg per dose
moa: Budesonide reduces airway inflammation; formoterol is a fast-onset, long-acting β2 agonist (12 h).
uses:
- Moderate–severe persistent asthma (maintenance; also as-needed reliever "MART" regimen)
- COPD with exacerbations
dose:
- Asthma: 1–2 inhalations twice daily; MART — 1 inhalation as needed in addition (max 8–12/day)
- As-needed only (mild asthma, GINA track 1): 1 inhalation when needed
ci:
- LABA must never be used without an inhaled steroid in asthma
se:
- Oral thrush, hoarseness, tremor, palpitations, headache
ddi:
- Ritonavir / itraconazole :: Budesonide levels rise → Cushing's syndrome :: Avoid; use beclometasone
- Non-selective beta-blockers :: Bronchospasm :: Avoid
food:
- None significant
preg: Budesonide has the most pregnancy safety data among ICS.
lact: Compatible.
monitor:
- Asthma control, exacerbations, technique
advice:
- Rinse mouth after use; breathe in fast and deep with dry-powder inhalers
storage: Below 30°C, cap on, keep dry.
pearls:
- LABA monotherapy in asthma increases asthma deaths — always with ICS

=== drug: ipratropium
name: Ipratropium bromide
class: Short-acting muscarinic antagonist (SAMA)
group: Respiratory
nemlit: S (aerosol 20 mcg/dose; nebuliser 250–500 mcg)
forms: Metered-dose inhaler 20 mcg/puff; nebuliser solution 250 mcg/mL
moa: Blocks muscarinic (M3) receptors in airway smooth muscle → bronchodilation; reduces mucus secretion.
uses:
- Acute severe asthma (with salbutamol nebulisation)
- COPD symptom relief
dose:
- Acute asthma: 500 mcg nebulised every 20 min × 3 with salbutamol, then 4–6 hourly (children 250 mcg)
- COPD: 20–40 mcg (1–2 puffs) 3–4 times daily
ci:
- Hypersensitivity to atropine derivatives; caution in angle-closure glaucoma, urinary retention/BPH
se:
- Dry mouth, cough, urinary retention, constipation, acute angle-closure glaucoma if nebulised mist enters eyes
ddi:
- Other anticholinergics (amitriptyline, hyoscine, chlorpheniramine) :: Additive anticholinergic effects :: Caution in elderly
food:
