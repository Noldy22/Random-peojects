# Drug monographs — cardiovascular.
# Format: "=== drug: id" starts an entry; "key: value" scalars; "key:" then "- item" lists;
# interaction items are "Partner :: effect :: what to do".

=== drug: hydrochlorothiazide
name: Hydrochlorothiazide (HCTZ)
class: Thiazide diuretic
group: Cardiovascular
nemlit: D (tablet 12.5 mg, 25 mg); bendroflumethiazide 5 mg is level A
forms: Tablet 12.5 mg, 25 mg; fixed-dose combinations with losartan/candesartan/telmisartan/irbesartan
moa: Inhibits the Na+/Cl- co-transporter in the distal convoluted tubule → natriuresis and diuresis; long-term BP fall is from reduced peripheral vascular resistance.
uses:
- First-line antihypertensive when there is no compelling indication (STG 7th Ed.)
- Isolated systolic hypertension; secondary prevention after stroke (with an ACE inhibitor)
- Mild oedema (heart failure, add-on to loop diuretic for resistant oedema)
dose:
- Hypertension: 12.5–25 mg once daily in the morning (doses above 25 mg add little BP effect but more metabolic side effects)
- Oedema: 25–50 mg once daily
ci:
- Anuria; severe renal impairment (eGFR <30 — ineffective)
- Refractory hypokalaemia, hyponatraemia, hypercalcaemia
- Symptomatic hyperuricaemia / history of gout (relative)
- Sulfonamide allergy (cross-sensitivity possible)
se:
- Hypokalaemia, hyponatraemia, hypomagnesaemia, hypercalcaemia
- Hyperuricaemia (can precipitate gout), hyperglycaemia, raised lipids
- Postural hypotension, dizziness, erectile dysfunction
- Photosensitivity rash; rarely pancreatitis, blood dyscrasias
ddi:
- Digoxin :: Thiazide-induced hypokalaemia increases digoxin toxicity :: Monitor K+; keep K+ ≥4 mmol/L
- Lithium :: Reduced lithium clearance → lithium toxicity :: Avoid or halve lithium dose and monitor levels
- NSAIDs :: Blunt the antihypertensive/diuretic effect; ↑ risk of AKI :: Avoid regular NSAIDs; use paracetamol
- Corticosteroids / salbutamol (high dose) :: Additive hypokalaemia :: Monitor potassium
- Antidiabetic drugs :: Thiazides raise blood glucose :: Monitor glucose; dose adjust if needed
food:
- Alcohol :: Additive postural hypotension :: Limit alcohol; rise slowly
- High-salt diet :: Reduces effectiveness :: Salt <5 g/day
- Potassium-rich foods (bananas, oranges, beans) :: Help offset hypokalaemia :: Encourage
preg: Avoid for gestational hypertension (reduces plasma volume); not first choice in pregnancy.
lact: Low doses acceptable; high doses may suppress lactation.
renal: Ineffective when eGFR <30 mL/min — use a loop diuretic.
monitor:
- BP, serum electrolytes (K+, Na+) and creatinine at baseline and 2–4 weeks after starting
- Uric acid and glucose, especially in gout or diabetes
advice:
- Take in the morning so the diuresis does not disturb sleep
- Stand up slowly; report muscle cramps, weakness or palpitations (low potassium)
- Use sunscreen — skin may burn more easily
- Continue lifestyle measures; do not stop without advice even when you feel well
storage: Below 25°C, protect from moisture.
pearls:
- STG 7th Ed. makes a thiazide the default first-line agent for uncomplicated hypertension (6th Ed. favoured CCB or thiazide in Black patients)
- Metabolic effects are dose-related — "low dose is the right dose"

=== drug: furosemide
name: Furosemide
class: Loop diuretic
group: Cardiovascular
nemlit: B (tablet 40 mg; injection 10 mg/mL)
forms: Tablet 40 mg; injection 10 mg/mL (2 mL ampoule)
moa: Blocks the Na+/K+/2Cl- co-transporter in the thick ascending limb of the loop of Henle → powerful, short-acting diuresis; IV also causes early venodilatation.
uses:
- Acute pulmonary oedema and acute decompensated heart failure
- Oedema of heart failure, CKD, nephrotic syndrome, liver cirrhosis (with spironolactone)
- Hypertension with fluid overload or CKD (eGFR <30); hypercalcaemia (after rehydration)
dose:
- Oedema: 20–40 mg PO once daily in the morning, titrate up to 80–120 mg/day (higher in CKD)
- STG 7th Ed. heart failure/hypertension: initial 40 mg PO twice daily, adjust to congestion
- Acute pulmonary oedema: 40–80 mg IV slowly (≤4 mg/min), repeat according to response
- Children: 1 mg/kg PO/IV (max 40 mg) 1–2 times daily
ci:
- Anuria; severe hypovolaemia or dehydration
- Hepatic encephalopathy / pre-coma
- Severe hypokalaemia or hyponatraemia
se:
- Hypokalaemia, hyponatraemia, hypomagnesaemia, hypocalcaemia, metabolic alkalosis
- Dehydration, hypotension, pre-renal AKI
- Hyperuricaemia/gout, hyperglycaemia
- Ototoxicity (tinnitus, deafness) with rapid IV doses or with aminoglycosides
ddi:
- Gentamicin / other aminoglycosides :: Additive ototoxicity and nephrotoxicity :: Avoid combination or monitor closely; slow IV furosemide
- Digoxin :: Hypokalaemia potentiates digoxin toxicity :: Monitor K+; supplement or add spironolactone
- Lithium :: Increased lithium levels :: Monitor lithium levels
- NSAIDs :: Reduced diuretic effect; ↑ AKI risk ("triple whammy" with ACE inhibitor) :: Avoid
- ACE inhibitors :: First-dose hypotension in volume-depleted patients :: Start ACE inhibitor low; consider holding diuretic 24 h
food:
- Alcohol :: Enhanced hypotension :: Limit
- Potassium-rich foods :: Help prevent hypokalaemia :: Encourage unless on K+-sparing drugs or in CKD
preg: Avoid unless clearly needed (may reduce placental perfusion); not for gestational hypertension.
lact: May suppress lactation; use with care.
renal: Effective at low eGFR but higher doses are needed.
hepatic: Can precipitate hepatic encephalopathy through hypokalaemia — combine with spironolactone in cirrhosis.
monitor:
- Weight daily (target loss 0.5–1 kg/day in oedema), fluid balance, BP
- Electrolytes and creatinine
advice:
- Take in the morning (second dose no later than mid-afternoon) to avoid night-time urination
- Weigh yourself daily at the same time; report gain of >2 kg in 3 days
- Report dizziness, severe thirst, muscle cramps, ringing in ears
storage: Below 25°C; protect injection from light (do not use if discoloured).
pearls:
- Onset ~1 h orally (lasts ~6 h — "furo-six"), ~5 min IV
- Rapid IV injection → ototoxicity; give no faster than 4 mg/min

=== drug: spironolactone
name: Spironolactone
class: Mineralocorticoid (aldosterone) receptor antagonist; potassium-sparing diuretic
group: Cardiovascular
nemlit: C (tablet 12.5 mg, 25 mg)
forms: Tablet 12.5 mg, 25 mg
moa: Competitive antagonist of aldosterone at the distal tubule/collecting duct → Na+ excretion and K+ retention; reduces myocardial fibrosis in heart failure.
uses:
- Heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (mortality benefit)
- Ascites due to cirrhosis (with furosemide in a 100:40 mg ratio)
- Resistant hypertension (step 4); primary hyperaldosteronism
- Nephrotic syndrome oedema
dose:
- Heart failure: 12.5–25 mg once daily (max 50 mg)
- Resistant hypertension: 25–50 mg once daily (STG: 25 mg OD)
- Ascites: 100 mg once daily, increase every 3–5 days to max 400 mg (with furosemide 40–160 mg)
ci:
- Hyperkalaemia (K+ >5.0 mmol/L); Addison's disease
- Severe renal impairment (eGFR <30) / anuria
- Concomitant potassium supplements or other K+-sparing diuretics (unless monitored)
se:
- Hyperkalaemia (can be fatal), hyponatraemia
- Gynaecomastia, breast tenderness, menstrual irregularity, impotence (anti-androgenic)
- GI upset, dizziness, rash
ddi:
- ACE inhibitors / ARBs :: Additive hyperkalaemia :: Monitor K+ and creatinine closely (week 1, 4, then regularly)
- Potassium chloride / K+ supplements / salt substitutes :: Severe hyperkalaemia :: Avoid
- NSAIDs :: Hyperkalaemia and AKI :: Avoid
- Co-trimoxazole (trimethoprim) :: Hyperkalaemia :: Monitor K+
- Digoxin :: Spironolactone may raise digoxin levels :: Monitor
food:
- Potassium-rich foods / "low sodium" salt substitutes (KCl) :: Risk of hyperkalaemia :: Avoid excessive intake and salt substitutes
- Food :: Increases absorption :: Take consistently with food
preg: Avoid (anti-androgenic effects on male fetus).
lact: Metabolite (canrenone) in milk — generally considered compatible, use with caution.
renal: Avoid if eGFR <30; reduce dose/monitor closely if 30–50.
monitor:
- K+ and creatinine before starting, at 1 and 4 weeks, then every 3–6 months
- Stop or halve dose if K+ >5.5 mmol/L
advice:
- Avoid salt substitutes and potassium supplements
- Report breast swelling or tenderness, muscle weakness, irregular heartbeat
- Take with food at the same time daily
storage: Below 25°C.
pearls:
- Classic pair in cirrhotic ascites: spironolactone 100 mg + furosemide 40 mg (keeps K+ balanced)
- Eplerenone is an alternative without gynaecomastia (level S)

=== drug: enalapril
name: Enalapril
class: Angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitor
group: Cardiovascular
nemlit: C (tablet 2.5 mg, 5 mg); captopril B, lisinopril C
forms: Tablet 2.5 mg, 5 mg (10 mg, 20 mg in some brands)
moa: Pro-drug converted to enalaprilat; blocks conversion of angiotensin I to angiotensin II and breakdown of bradykinin → vasodilatation, ↓ aldosterone, ↓ cardiac remodelling and intraglomerular pressure.
uses:
- Hypertension, particularly with diabetes, CKD, heart failure, LVH or post-MI
- Heart failure with reduced EF (mortality benefit)
- Diabetic nephropathy / proteinuric CKD (renoprotection)
- Secondary prevention after MI and stroke
dose:
- Hypertension: start 5 mg once daily (2.5 mg if on diuretic, elderly or renal impairment); usual 10–20 mg daily; STG lists 10 mg twice daily
- Heart failure: 2.5 mg twice daily, titrate every 2 weeks to 10–20 mg twice daily
ci:
- Pregnancy (fetotoxic: renal failure, oligohydramnios, skull defects)
- History of angioedema (with any ACE inhibitor) or hereditary angioedema
- Bilateral renal artery stenosis (or stenosis in a single kidney)
- Combination with an ARB or aliskiren
se:
- Dry persistent cough (~10%, bradykinin-mediated)
- First-dose hypotension, dizziness
- Hyperkalaemia, rise in creatinine (accept up to 30%)
- Angioedema (rare but life-threatening — more common in Black patients), rash, taste disturbance
ddi:
- Spironolactone / K+ supplements / potassium-sparing diuretics :: Hyperkalaemia :: Monitor K+; avoid KCl supplements
- NSAIDs :: Reduced antihypertensive effect; AKI ("triple whammy" with diuretic) :: Avoid NSAIDs
- ARBs :: Dual RAAS blockade — hyperkalaemia, AKI, no extra benefit :: Never combine
- Lithium :: Raised lithium levels :: Monitor levels
- Diuretics :: Profound first-dose hypotension :: Start low, take first dose at bedtime
- Co-trimoxazole :: Hyperkalaemia :: Monitor K+
food:
- Potassium-rich foods / salt substitutes :: Hyperkalaemia :: Avoid salt substitutes
- Alcohol :: Additive hypotension :: Limit
preg: CONTRAINDICATED — switch to methyldopa, labetalol or nifedipine before conception or as soon as pregnancy is confirmed.
lact: Enalapril and captopril are considered acceptable during breastfeeding.
renal: Reduce starting dose (2.5 mg) if eGFR <30; monitor K+ and creatinine.
monitor:
- BP; serum creatinine and K+ before starting and 1–2 weeks after each dose increase
- Stop if creatinine rises >30% or K+ >6 mmol/L
advice:
- Take the first dose at bedtime; rise slowly
- A tickly dry cough is common — report it (an ARB can replace it)
- Seek urgent care for swelling of lips, tongue, face or difficulty breathing
- Women: use effective contraception; tell your clinician immediately if pregnant
- Avoid painkillers like ibuprofen/diclofenac without advice
storage: Below 25°C, dry place.
pearls:
- If cough → switch to ARB (losartan); if angioedema → never rechallenge with any ACE inhibitor
- ACE inhibitors are renoprotective in diabetes even when normotensive with microalbuminuria

=== drug: captopril
name: Captopril
class: ACE inhibitor (short-acting)
group: Cardiovascular
nemlit: B (tablet 12.5 mg, 25 mg)
forms: Tablet 12.5 mg, 25 mg
moa: Directly acting ACE inhibitor (not a pro-drug); short half-life needing 2–3 doses per day.
uses:
- Hypertension; hypertensive urgency (oral, gradual reduction)
- Heart failure; post-MI; diabetic nephropathy
dose:
- Hypertension: 6.25–25 mg every 8 hours (STG 7th Ed.); max 50 mg 8–12 hourly
- Heart failure: test dose 6.25 mg, then titrate to 25–50 mg three times daily
ci:
- Pregnancy; angioedema history; bilateral renal artery stenosis; combination with ARB
se:
- Cough, first-dose hypotension, hyperkalaemia, taste disturbance (metallic), rash, neutropenia (rare), angioedema
ddi:
- Spironolactone / K+ supplements :: Hyperkalaemia :: Monitor K+
- NSAIDs :: Reduced effect, AKI :: Avoid
- Lithium :: Lithium toxicity :: Monitor levels
- Diuretics :: First-dose hypotension :: Start low
food:
- Food :: Reduces absorption by ~30–40% :: Take 1 hour before meals
- Salt substitutes (KCl) :: Hyperkalaemia :: Avoid
preg: CONTRAINDICATED.
lact: Compatible.
renal: Reduce dose and frequency; monitor K+/creatinine.
monitor:
- BP, K+, creatinine; FBC in renal impairment or collagen disease (neutropenia)
advice:
- Take on an empty stomach, 1 hour before food
- Same cough/angioedema/pregnancy counselling as other ACE inhibitors
storage: Below 25°C; may smell faintly of sulphur (normal).
pearls:
- Only ACE inhibitor that must be taken on an empty stomach and has a sulfhydryl group (taste disturbance, rash)

=== drug: losartan
name: Losartan
class: Angiotensin II receptor blocker (ARB)
group: Cardiovascular
nemlit: C (tablet 50 mg; fixed combination with HCTZ); candesartan, telmisartan, irbesartan S
forms: Tablet 25 mg, 50 mg, 100 mg; losartan/HCTZ 50/12.5 mg
moa: Blocks angiotensin II at AT1 receptors → vasodilatation and ↓ aldosterone; does not raise bradykinin (no cough).
uses:
- Hypertension (alternative when ACE inhibitor not tolerated)
- Diabetic nephropathy, proteinuric CKD; heart failure (when ACE inhibitor not tolerated); LVH
dose:
- Hypertension: 50 mg once daily (25 mg in elderly/volume depletion), max 100 mg once daily
- Heart failure: 12.5–25 mg once daily, titrate to 50–150 mg
ci:
- Pregnancy; bilateral renal artery stenosis; combination with ACE inhibitor or aliskiren
se:
- Dizziness, hypotension, hyperkalaemia, raised creatinine; angioedema (much rarer than ACE inhibitors)
ddi:
- ACE inhibitors :: Dual RAAS blockade — never combine :: Avoid
- Spironolactone / K+ supplements :: Hyperkalaemia :: Monitor K+
- NSAIDs :: AKI and reduced effect :: Avoid
- Lithium :: Lithium toxicity :: Monitor levels
- Rifampicin / fluconazole :: Rifampicin lowers, fluconazole raises active metabolite levels :: Monitor BP
food:
- Salt substitutes / high-potassium diet :: Hyperkalaemia :: Avoid salt substitutes
- Grapefruit juice :: May alter losartan metabolism :: Avoid large amounts
preg: CONTRAINDICATED.
lact: Avoid (limited data) — prefer enalapril.
renal: No dose change needed but monitor K+/creatinine.
hepatic: Start with 25 mg in hepatic impairment.
monitor:
- BP, K+, creatinine at baseline and 1–2 weeks after starting/titrating
advice:
- Can be taken with or without food, at the same time each day
- Rise slowly; avoid potassium supplements and salt substitutes
- Effective contraception; stop and report if pregnant
storage: Below 25°C.
pearls:
- Losartan has a mild uricosuric effect — useful in hypertensive patients with gout
- Never combine ARB + ACE inhibitor (ONTARGET)

=== drug: amlodipine
name: Amlodipine
class: Calcium channel blocker (dihydropyridine, long-acting)
group: Cardiovascular
nemlit: C (tablet 5 mg, 10 mg)
forms: Tablet 5 mg, 10 mg
moa: Blocks L-type calcium channels in vascular smooth muscle → arteriolar vasodilatation and ↓ peripheral resistance; minimal effect on cardiac conduction.
uses:
- Hypertension (preferred in elderly and isolated systolic hypertension)
- Chronic stable angina
dose:
- Adults: 5 mg once daily, increase to 10 mg once daily after 1–2 weeks
- Elderly/hepatic impairment: start 2.5 mg
ci:
- Cardiogenic shock, severe aortic stenosis, unstable angina (as monotherapy)
se:
- Ankle (peripheral) oedema — dose-related, not due to fluid retention (does not respond to diuretics)
- Flushing, headache, palpitations, dizziness
- Gingival hyperplasia (long-term), fatigue
ddi:
- Simvastatin :: CYP3A4 inhibition raises simvastatin → myopathy :: Limit simvastatin to 20 mg/day or use atorvastatin
- CYP3A4 inhibitors (clarithromycin, ritonavir, itraconazole) :: Raised amlodipine levels → hypotension, oedema :: Monitor; reduce dose
- CYP3A4 inducers (rifampicin, carbamazepine, phenytoin) :: Reduced amlodipine effect :: Monitor BP; dose may need increase
- Other antihypertensives :: Additive hypotension :: Monitor
food:
- Grapefruit juice :: Modest increase in levels (less than with felodipine/nifedipine) :: Avoid large quantities
- Alcohol :: Additive hypotension :: Limit
preg: Limited data; nifedipine is preferred in pregnancy.
lact: Small amounts in milk; generally acceptable.
hepatic: Start 2.5 mg; titrate slowly.
monitor:
- BP, heart rate, ankle swelling
advice:
- Once daily at the same time, with or without food
- Ankle swelling is common — report if troublesome (dose change or adding ACE inhibitor helps)
- Maintain good oral hygiene (gum overgrowth)
storage: Below 25°C, protect from light.
pearls:
- Long half-life (35–50 h): a missed dose has little effect; steady state after 7–8 days
- CCB oedema improves when an ACE inhibitor/ARB is added (venodilatation)

=== drug: nifedipine
name: Nifedipine
class: Calcium channel blocker (dihydropyridine)
group: Cardiovascular
nemlit: B (slow-release 10 mg, 20 mg; immediate-release 10 mg, 20 mg)
forms: Slow-release (retard) tablet 10 mg, 20 mg (also 30–90 mg once-daily forms); immediate-release capsule 10 mg
moa: L-type calcium channel blocker → arteriolar vasodilatation; also relaxes uterine smooth muscle (tocolysis).
uses:
- Hypertension, including in pregnancy (slow-release) and severe hypertension in pregnancy (oral)
- Angina (slow-release only)
- Tocolysis in preterm labour; Raynaud's phenomenon
dose:
- Hypertension: slow-release 10–20 mg twice daily (max 40 mg twice daily) or once-daily form 30–90 mg
- Severe hypertension in pregnancy: 10 mg orally, repeat after 30 min if needed (STG/WHO)
- Tocolysis: 20 mg orally, then 10–20 mg every 6–8 h
ci:
- Cardiogenic shock; within 1 month of MI; unstable angina
- Immediate-release forms for chronic hypertension or angina (reflex tachycardia, ischaemia)
se:
- Headache, flushing, palpitations/reflex tachycardia, ankle oedema, dizziness, gum hyperplasia
ddi:
- Magnesium sulfate :: Profound hypotension and neuromuscular blockade :: Use together only with close monitoring
- CYP3A4 inhibitors (clarithromycin, ritonavir, azoles) :: Raised nifedipine levels :: Monitor/avoid
- Rifampicin, phenytoin, carbamazepine :: Markedly reduced effect :: Avoid; choose another agent
- Beta-blockers :: Additive hypotension (but useful combination) :: Monitor
food:
- Grapefruit juice :: Marked increase in nifedipine levels → hypotension :: Avoid grapefruit
- Alcohol :: Additive hypotension :: Avoid
preg: Used for hypertension and tocolysis in pregnancy (avoid in first trimester if possible).
lact: Compatible.
monitor:
- BP, heart rate, ankle oedema
advice:
- Swallow slow-release tablets whole — do not crush or chew
- Never bite a capsule under the tongue (sublingual use causes dangerous BP drops)
- Avoid grapefruit juice
storage: Protect from light (light-sensitive).
pearls:
- Sublingual nifedipine is NOT recommended for hypertensive emergencies (uncontrolled fall in BP → stroke/MI)

=== drug: atenolol
name: Atenolol
class: Beta-1 selective adrenoceptor blocker
group: Cardiovascular
nemlit: B (tablet 50 mg)
forms: Tablet 25 mg, 50 mg, 100 mg
moa: Competitively blocks cardiac β1 receptors → ↓ heart rate, contractility and renin release; water-soluble, renally excreted.
uses:
- Hypertension (with compelling indication: angina, post-MI, tachyarrhythmia)
- Angina; rate control in atrial fibrillation; migraine prophylaxis; anxiety symptoms
dose:
- Hypertension: 25–50 mg once daily (max 100 mg)
- Angina: 50–100 mg daily
ci:
- Asthma (bronchospasm), 2nd/3rd degree heart block, sick sinus syndrome
- Bradycardia (<50/min), cardiogenic shock, decompensated heart failure
- Severe peripheral arterial disease; untreated phaeochromocytoma
se:
- Bradycardia, fatigue, cold extremities, dizziness
- Bronchospasm (less than non-selective), sleep disturbance, erectile dysfunction
- Masks symptoms of hypoglycaemia (tremor, palpitations)
ddi:
- Verapamil / diltiazem :: Severe bradycardia, heart block, asystole :: Never combine (especially IV verapamil)
- Insulin / sulfonylureas :: Masked hypoglycaemia warning signs :: Educate patient; monitor glucose
- Amiodarone / digoxin :: Additive bradycardia :: Monitor heart rate
- NSAIDs :: Reduced antihypertensive effect :: Avoid regular use
- Clonidine :: Rebound hypertension if clonidine stopped while on β-blocker :: Stop β-blocker first
food:
- Orange/apple juice :: Can reduce atenolol absorption :: Take with water
- Alcohol :: Additive hypotension :: Limit
preg: Avoid (fetal growth restriction) — labetalol is preferred in pregnancy.
lact: Accumulates in breast milk — avoid; prefer propranolol or labetalol.
renal: Reduce dose if eGFR <35 (renally cleared).
monitor:
- Heart rate (hold if <50–55/min), BP, signs of heart failure
advice:
- Never stop suddenly — rebound angina, tachycardia, or MI; taper over 1–2 weeks
- Diabetics: sweating may be the only warning of low sugar — check glucose
- May cause tiredness and cold hands
storage: Below 25°C.
pearls:
- Beta-blockers are no longer first-line for uncomplicated hypertension (less stroke protection)

=== drug: bisoprolol
name: Bisoprolol
class: Beta-1 selective adrenoceptor blocker
group: Cardiovascular
nemlit: S (tablet 5 mg, 10 mg)
forms: Tablet 1.25 mg, 2.5 mg, 5 mg, 10 mg
moa: Highly β1-selective blocker; reduces heart rate, myocardial oxygen demand and neurohormonal activation in heart failure.
uses:
- Chronic heart failure with reduced EF (mortality benefit) — once stable
- Hypertension (with compelling indication), angina, AF rate control
dose:
- Heart failure: 1.25 mg once daily, double every 2–4 weeks as tolerated to 10 mg once daily ("start low, go slow")
- Hypertension/angina: 2.5–10 mg once daily (STG)
ci:
- Acute decompensated heart failure, cardiogenic shock, symptomatic bradycardia, 2nd/3rd degree AV block, severe asthma
se:
- Bradycardia, hypotension, fatigue, transient worsening of heart failure on initiation, dizziness, cold extremities
ddi:
- Verapamil / diltiazem :: Heart block, severe bradycardia :: Avoid
- Amiodarone / digoxin :: Bradycardia :: Monitor heart rate
- Insulin / sulfonylureas :: Masked hypoglycaemia :: Monitor glucose
- NSAIDs :: Reduced effect :: Avoid
food:
- Alcohol :: Additive hypotension :: Limit
preg: Use only if benefit outweighs risk (fetal growth restriction); labetalol preferred.
lact: Avoid (no data).
monitor:
- Heart rate, BP, weight and symptoms of heart failure during titration
advice:
- In heart failure you may feel more tired for the first weeks — this improves; do not stop
- Weigh daily; report weight gain >2 kg in 3 days
- Never stop suddenly
storage: Below 25°C.
pearls:
- Evidence-based β-blockers for HFrEF: bisoprolol, carvedilol, metoprolol succinate (not atenolol)

=== drug: carvedilol
name: Carvedilol
class: Non-selective beta-blocker with alpha-1 blocking activity
group: Cardiovascular
nemlit: C (tablet 6.25 mg, 12.5 mg)
forms: Tablet 3.125 mg, 6.25 mg, 12.5 mg, 25 mg
moa: Blocks β1, β2 and α1 receptors → ↓ heart rate and vasodilatation; antioxidant effects.
uses:
- Heart failure with reduced EF (with ACE inhibitor + diuretic)
- Hypertension; post-MI LV dysfunction
dose:
- Heart failure: 3.125 mg twice daily, double every 2 weeks to target 25 mg twice daily (50 mg BD if >85 kg)
- Hypertension: 6.25–12.5 mg once or twice daily (STG), max 25 mg twice daily
ci:
- Asthma/bronchospasm, decompensated HF needing IV inotropes, severe bradycardia, AV block, severe hepatic impairment
se:
- Dizziness, postural hypotension, bradycardia, fatigue, weight gain/oedema, bronchospasm, hyperglycaemia masking
ddi:
- Verapamil / diltiazem :: Heart block :: Avoid
- Digoxin :: Raised digoxin levels and bradycardia :: Monitor
- Insulin / sulfonylureas :: Masked hypoglycaemia :: Monitor glucose
- Rifampicin :: Reduced carvedilol levels :: Monitor BP/HR
food:
- Food :: Slows absorption and reduces postural hypotension :: Take with food
- Alcohol :: Additive hypotension :: Limit
preg: Use only if essential; labetalol preferred.
lact: Avoid.
hepatic: Contraindicated in severe hepatic impairment.
monitor:
- HR, BP (lying/standing), weight, symptoms during titration
advice:
- Take with food to reduce dizziness; rise slowly
- Do not stop suddenly
storage: Below 25°C, protect from moisture.
pearls:
- Alpha-blockade makes postural hypotension more likely than with selective β-blockers

=== drug: propranolol
name: Propranolol
class: Non-selective beta-blocker (lipophilic)
group: Cardiovascular
nemlit: A (tablet 40 mg)
forms: Tablet 10 mg, 40 mg, 80 mg
moa: Blocks β1 and β2 receptors; lipophilic (crosses blood–brain barrier); reduces portal pressure via ↓ cardiac output and splanchnic vasoconstriction.
uses:
- Primary and secondary prophylaxis of variceal bleeding in portal hypertension
- Thyrotoxicosis symptoms (and thyroid storm), migraine prophylaxis, essential tremor, anxiety (somatic symptoms)
- Hypertension (STG 40 mg OD), angina, arrhythmias; infantile haemangioma
dose:
- Portal hypertension: 20–40 mg twice daily, titrate to reduce resting HR by 25% (or to ~55–60/min)
- Thyrotoxicosis: 10–40 mg three to four times daily
- Migraine prophylaxis: 40 mg two to three times daily (80–160 mg/day)
- Hypertension: 40 mg once to twice daily (max 160 mg twice daily)
ci:
- Asthma or history of bronchospasm (absolute), COPD with reversibility
- Bradycardia, AV block, cardiogenic shock, decompensated HF, severe peripheral vascular disease, Prinzmetal angina
se:
- Bronchospasm, bradycardia, cold extremities, fatigue, vivid dreams/nightmares, depression, erectile dysfunction
- Masks hypoglycaemia; worsens peripheral vascular disease
ddi:
- Verapamil / diltiazem :: Heart block, asystole :: Avoid
- Insulin / sulfonylureas :: Prolonged and masked hypoglycaemia :: Monitor glucose
- Salbutamol :: Antagonises bronchodilator effect :: Avoid in asthma
- Rifampicin :: Reduced propranolol levels :: Monitor
- Lidocaine :: Raised lidocaine levels :: Monitor
food:
- Food :: Increases bioavailability :: Take consistently with meals
- Alcohol :: Additive hypotension and CNS effects :: Limit
preg: Can be used (e.g. thyrotoxicosis) but may cause fetal growth restriction and neonatal bradycardia/hypoglycaemia.
lact: Compatible.
hepatic: High first-pass metabolism — reduce dose in cirrhosis.
monitor:
- Heart rate, BP; in portal hypertension aim for HR ~55–60/min
advice:
- Never stop suddenly (rebound tachycardia/angina, variceal bleed risk)
- Tell any prescriber you have asthma — propranolol must not be used
- May cause vivid dreams and cold hands
storage: Below 25°C, protect from light.
pearls:
- Drug of choice for symptoms of thyrotoxicosis (also blocks peripheral T4→T3 conversion)

=== drug: labetalol
name: Labetalol
class: Combined alpha-1 and non-selective beta blocker
group: Cardiovascular
nemlit: C (tablet 100 mg, 200 mg; injection 5 mg/mL)
forms: Tablet 100 mg, 200 mg; injection 5 mg/mL
