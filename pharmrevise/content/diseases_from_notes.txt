=== disease: hypertension
name: Essential Hypertension
category: Cardiovascular
stg: Ch. 20 — Cardiovascular (§20.5)
notes: Cardiovascular_Disorders_Study_Notes#1
quickref: Essential Hypertension
aka: high blood pressure; HTN; shinikizo la damu
investigations:
- BP measured correctly on 3 occasions (seated, arm at heart level, correct cuff size)
- Urinalysis (protein, blood), serum creatinine/eGFR, electrolytes (K+), fasting glucose/HbA1c, lipid profile
- ECG (LVH), fundoscopy; echocardiogram if LVH or heart failure suspected
advice:
- Hypertension is usually silent — keep taking your tablets even when you feel well
- Reduce salt (no added salt at the table, avoid salty snacks, dagaa and processed foods), eat more vegetables and fruit
- Walk briskly 30 minutes most days; aim for a healthy weight; stop smoking; limit alcohol
- Check your BP regularly and bring your readings to clinic
- Avoid painkillers like ibuprofen/diclofenac and decongestant cold remedies without advice (they raise BP)
- ACE inhibitor/ARB users: use contraception, report dry cough or lip/face swelling
pearls:
- STG 7th Ed: thiazide diuretic is the default first-line drug when no compelling indication
- Start two drugs when BP is >20/10 mmHg above target
- Never combine an ACE inhibitor with an ARB
- Pregnancy: labetalol, nifedipine, methyldopa, hydralazine — never ACEi/ARB
danger:
- BP ≥180/110 with headache, chest pain, breathlessness, confusion, weakness or visual loss → hypertensive emergency, refer
drugs: hydrochlorothiazide, amlodipine, enalapril, captopril, losartan, atenolol, bisoprolol, carvedilol, methyldopa, labetalol, nifedipine, spironolactone, furosemide, hydralazine

=== disease: coronary-heart-disease
name: Coronary Heart Disease (Angina & Acute Coronary Syndrome)
category: Cardiovascular
stg: Ch. 20 (§20.1–20.4)
notes: Cardiovascular_Disorders_Study_Notes#2
quickref: Coronary Heart Disease
aka: ischaemic heart disease; IHD; angina; myocardial infarction; heart attack; ACS; STEMI; NSTEMI
investigations:
- 12-lead ECG within 10 minutes of arrival (ST elevation/depression, T inversion, new LBBB)
- Cardiac troponin (repeat at 3–6 h), FBC, electrolytes, creatinine, glucose, lipids
- Echocardiography; exercise stress test or angiography for stable disease
advice:
- Keep GTN with you; sit down, use 1 spray under the tongue, repeat after 5 minutes; if pain persists 5 minutes after first dose call for emergency help
- Take aspirin/clopidogrel every day; never stop after a stent without talking to your cardiologist
- Statin daily; stop smoking completely; healthy diet; gradual return to exercise
- Do not use sildenafil-type medicines with nitrates
pearls:
- ACS immediate care: aspirin 300 mg chewed + clopidogrel loading, oxygen only if SpO2 <90%, GTN, analgesia (morphine), anticoagulation, reperfusion
- Long-term "ABCS": Antiplatelet, Beta-blocker, ACE inhibitor, Statin
- Avoid NSAIDs and non-dihydropyridine CCBs in heart failure after MI
danger:
- Chest pain >20 minutes at rest, sweating, breathlessness, syncope → emergency referral
drugs: aspirin, clopidogrel, glyceryl-trinitrate, atorvastatin, atenolol, bisoprolol, enalapril, heparin, enoxaparin, morphine, alteplase, simvastatin

=== disease: pregnancy-hypertension
name: Hypertensive Disorders of Pregnancy (Pre-eclampsia & Eclampsia)
category: Obstetrics & Gynaecology
stg: Ch. 11 (§11.7.2–11.7.5)
notes: Cardiovascular_Disorders_Study_Notes#3
quickref: Pregnancy-Induced Hypertension (and Pre-Eclampsia/Eclampsia)
aka: pregnancy-induced hypertension; PIH; pre-eclampsia; eclampsia; HELLP; gestational hypertension
investigations:
- BP every visit; urine protein (dipstick ≥2+ or PCR ≥0.3)
- FBC with platelets, LFTs (ALT/AST), creatinine, uric acid, LDH and blood film (HELLP), clotting profile
- Fetal assessment: growth scan, Doppler, CTG
advice:
- Attend every antenatal visit; BP checks are vital in the second half of pregnancy
- Seek urgent care for severe headache, blurred vision/flashing lights, upper abdominal pain, sudden swelling of face/hands, reduced baby movements, or fits
- Low-dose aspirin at night from 12 weeks if high risk (as prescribed); calcium supplement where advised
- Methyldopa can cause drowsiness; switch is often made after delivery
pearls:
- MgSO4 is the anticonvulsant of choice (Pritchard: 4 g IV + 10 g IM, then 5 g IM 4-hourly); check reflexes, RR ≥16, urine ≥30 mL/h before each dose
- Antidote for magnesium toxicity: calcium gluconate 1 g IV over 10 min
- Definitive treatment is delivery; give dexamethasone 6 mg IM 12-hourly × 4 if <34 weeks
- ACE inhibitors/ARBs are contraindicated in pregnancy
danger:
- BP ≥160/110, convulsions, oliguria, pulmonary oedema, platelets <100 → severe pre-eclampsia/eclampsia — stabilise and refer
drugs: magnesium-sulfate, methyldopa, labetalol, nifedipine, hydralazine, calcium-gluconate, aspirin, dexamethasone

=== disease: heart-failure
name: Heart Failure
category: Cardiovascular
stg: Ch. 20 (§20.9–20.11)
notes: Cardiovascular_Disorders_Study_Notes#4
quickref: Heart Failure
aka: congestive cardiac failure; CCF; HFrEF; HFpEF; pulmonary oedema
investigations:
- Echocardiography (ejection fraction), ECG, chest X-ray (cardiomegaly, Kerley B lines, effusions)
- BNP/NT-proBNP where available; FBC (anaemia), electrolytes, creatinine, TFTs, glucose
advice:
- Weigh yourself every morning; report a gain of more than 2 kg in 3 days or increasing ankle swelling/breathlessness
- Limit salt; follow fluid advice (often 1.5–2 L/day if advanced)
- Take furosemide in the morning; keep taking beta-blocker and ACE inhibitor even if you feel tired at first
- Avoid NSAIDs (ibuprofen, diclofenac) — they cause fluid retention and worsen heart failure
- Get influenza/pneumococcal vaccination where available
pearls:
- Mortality-reducing "pillars" in HFrEF: ACEi/ARB(/ARNI), evidence-based β-blocker, MRA (spironolactone), SGLT2 inhibitor
- Diuretics relieve symptoms only — no mortality benefit
- Avoid verapamil/diltiazem, NSAIDs, thiazolidinediones in HFrEF
- Digoxin toxicity is precipitated by hypokalaemia from diuretics
danger:
- Breathlessness at rest, pink frothy sputum, chest pain, fainting → acute pulmonary oedema emergency
drugs: furosemide, enalapril, captopril, losartan, bisoprolol, carvedilol, spironolactone, digoxin, glyceryl-trinitrate, hydralazine

=== disease: arrhythmias
name: Cardiac Arrhythmias (incl. Atrial Fibrillation)
category: Cardiovascular
stg: Ch. 20 (§20.15–20.17)
notes: Cardiovascular_Disorders_Study_Notes#5
quickref: Cardiac Arrhythmias
aka: atrial fibrillation; AF; SVT; ventricular tachycardia; heart block; bradycardia; palpitations
investigations:
- 12-lead ECG (and rhythm strip/Holter), electrolytes (K+, Mg2+, Ca2+), TFTs, echocardiogram
- Assess stroke risk (CHA2DS2-VASc) and bleeding risk (HAS-BLED) in AF
advice:
- Take anticoagulant at the same time daily and attend INR checks; carry an anticoagulant card
- Learn to check your pulse; report fainting, chest pain, breathlessness
- Limit alcohol and caffeine; treat thyroid disease
pearls:
- AF management = rate control (β-blocker, digoxin) + rhythm control (selected) + stroke prevention (warfarin/DOAC)
- Unstable tachyarrhythmia (shock, syncope, ischaemia, heart failure) → synchronised DC cardioversion
- Amiodarone: thyroid, lung, liver, skin toxicity and doubles digoxin/raises INR
danger:
- Palpitations with fainting, chest pain, hypotension or breathlessness → emergency
drugs: amiodarone, digoxin, bisoprolol, atenolol, propranolol, warfarin, heparin, aspirin

=== disease: peripheral-vascular
name: Peripheral Vascular Disorders (Peripheral Arterial Disease)
category: Cardiovascular
stg: Ch. 20
notes: Cardiovascular_Disorders_Study_Notes#6
quickref: Peripheral Vascular Disorders
aka: peripheral arterial disease; PAD; intermittent claudication; critical limb ischaemia
investigations:
- Ankle-brachial pressure index (ABPI <0.9 diagnostic), Doppler ultrasound, angiography before revascularisation
- Glucose/HbA1c, lipids, renal function
advice:
- Stop smoking completely — the single most important measure
- Walk daily until pain, rest, then continue (supervised exercise improves walking distance)
- Inspect feet daily (especially diabetics), wear well-fitting shoes, never walk barefoot
pearls:
- All patients need antiplatelet + statin + BP/glucose control
- Non-selective β-blockers may worsen claudication — use cautiously
danger:
- Sudden cold, pale, painful, pulseless limb → acute limb ischaemia (surgical emergency)
drugs: aspirin, clopidogrel, atorvastatin, enalapril, heparin

=== disease: dvt-pe
name: Venous Thromboembolism (DVT & Pulmonary Embolism)
category: Cardiovascular
stg: Ch. 3 (§3.3.1–3.3.2), Ch. 20 (§20.14)
notes: Cardiovascular_Disorders_Study_Notes#7
quickref: Thromboembolic Disorders — Deep Vein Thrombosis and Pulmonary Embolism
aka: deep vein thrombosis; DVT; pulmonary embolism; PE; VTE; blood clot
investigations:
- Wells score; D-dimer (to exclude if low probability); compression ultrasound (DVT)
- CT pulmonary angiography (PE); ECG (sinus tachycardia, S1Q3T3), echo for RV strain
- Baseline FBC, platelets, PT/INR, APTT, creatinine before anticoagulation
advice:
- Warfarin: same time daily, regular INR checks, consistent vitamin K intake, avoid NSAIDs and herbal remedies
- LMWH: inject into abdomen, rotate sites, do not rub
- Report bleeding, black stools, severe headache; chest pain or breathlessness
- On long journeys walk/stretch hourly and stay hydrated
pearls:
- Overlap heparin/LMWH with warfarin for ≥5 days AND until INR ≥2 on 2 consecutive days
- Provoked VTE: 3 months; unprovoked/recurrent: longer or indefinite; cancer: LMWH preferred
- Pregnancy: LMWH (warfarin is teratogenic)
- Antidotes: protamine (heparin), vitamin K ± FFP/PCC (warfarin)
danger:
- Sudden breathlessness, chest pain, coughing blood, collapse → possible PE (emergency)
drugs: heparin, enoxaparin, warfarin, alteplase, vitamin-k

=== disease: stroke
name: Stroke (Ischaemic & Haemorrhagic)
category: Neurology
stg: Ch. 8 (§8.2)
notes: Cardiovascular_Disorders_Study_Notes#8
quickref: Thromboembolic Disorders — Stroke
aka: CVA; cerebrovascular accident; brain attack; TIA; transient ischaemic attack; kiharusi
investigations:
- Urgent non-contrast CT head (to exclude haemorrhage before aspirin/thrombolysis)
- Blood glucose (exclude hypoglycaemia), FBC, clotting, electrolytes, lipids, ECG (AF), echo, carotid Doppler
advice:
- Recognise FAST: Face drooping, Arm weakness, Speech difficulty — Time to call for help
- Secondary prevention for life: antiplatelet (ischaemic), statin, BP control, stop smoking, control diabetes
- Swallow assessment before giving oral medicines/food; physiotherapy early
pearls:
- Thrombolysis (alteplase) within 4.5 h for ischaemic stroke only after CT excludes bleeding
- Aspirin 300 mg daily for 14 days after ischaemic stroke (once haemorrhage excluded), then long-term antiplatelet
- Nimodipine 60 mg 4-hourly for subarachnoid haemorrhage (prevents vasospasm)
- Permissive BP in acute ischaemic stroke (treat only if >220/120, or >185/110 if thrombolysing)
danger:
- Any sudden weakness, speech or vision loss → emergency referral (time is brain)
drugs: aspirin, clopidogrel, atorvastatin, alteplase, enalapril, hydrochlorothiazide, amlodipine, warfarin, heparin

=== disease: hypertensive-crisis
name: Hypertensive Crises (Urgency & Emergency)
category: Cardiovascular
stg: Ch. 20 (§20.6–20.8)
notes: Cardiovascular_Disorders_Study_Notes#9
quickref: Hypertensive Crises (Urgency and Emergency)
aka: hypertensive emergency; hypertensive urgency; malignant hypertension; hypertensive encephalopathy
investigations:
- Look for acute target-organ damage: fundoscopy, ECG, troponin, creatinine, urinalysis, CXR, CT head if neurological signs, pregnancy test
advice:
- After stabilisation, adherence to long-term antihypertensives prevents recurrence (non-adherence is the commonest cause)
pearls:
- Urgency (no organ damage): oral agents, lower over 24–48 h
- Emergency: IV labetalol/GTN/hydralazine; reduce MAP by ≤25% in the first hour
- Aortic dissection: β-blocker first, target SBP <120 within 20 min; phaeochromocytoma: α-blocker before β-blocker
- Never use sublingual nifedipine
danger:
- Severe BP with chest pain, breathlessness, confusion, seizure, weakness or pregnancy → emergency
drugs: labetalol, hydralazine, glyceryl-trinitrate, nifedipine, captopril, amlodipine, magnesium-sulfate, furosemide

=== disease: asthma
name: Asthma
category: Respiratory
stg: Ch. 9 (§9.2.1–9.2.2)
notes: Pulmonary_GI_Hepatic_Study_Notes#1
quickref: Asthma
aka: bronchial asthma; pumu; wheeze
investigations:
- Peak expiratory flow (variability >20%), spirometry with reversibility (FEV1 ↑ ≥12% and 200 mL after salbutamol)
- Acute: SpO2, PEF, ability to talk; CXR if pneumothorax/pneumonia suspected; blood gases in life-threatening asthma
advice:
- Preventer (steroid) inhaler every day; reliever only when needed — needing it >2 times a week means poor control
- Correct inhaler technique with a spacer; rinse mouth after steroid inhaler
- Avoid triggers: smoke, dust, cold air, aspirin/NSAIDs if sensitive, β-blockers
- Written action plan: when to increase treatment and when to go to hospital
pearls:
- Acute severe asthma: oxygen, salbutamol (± ipratropium) nebulised every 20 min, prednisolone 40–50 mg (or hydrocortisone IV), MgSO4 IV if poor response
- Every asthmatic needs an inhaled steroid-containing treatment (no SABA-only therapy)
- Propranolol and other non-selective β-blockers are contraindicated
danger:
- Cannot speak in sentences, drowsy, cyanosis, silent chest, PEF <33% → life-threatening asthma
drugs: salbutamol, beclometasone, budesonide-formoterol, ipratropium, prednisolone, hydrocortisone, aminophylline, magnesium-sulfate

=== disease: copd
name: Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD)
category: Respiratory
stg: Ch. 9 (§9.3–9.5)
notes: Pulmonary_GI_Hepatic_Study_Notes#2
quickref: Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease
aka: COPD; chronic bronchitis; emphysema
investigations:
- Spirometry (post-bronchodilator FEV1/FVC <0.7), CXR (hyperinflation, exclude TB/cancer), SpO2, FBC (polycythaemia)
- Sputum for AFB if chronic cough (TB is common in Tanzania)
advice:
- Stop smoking and avoid biomass/charcoal smoke — the only measures that slow decline
- Use inhalers daily with correct technique; pulmonary rehabilitation/exercise
- Get vaccinated (influenza, pneumococcal) where available
- Start rescue pack/seek care early when sputum increases, changes colour or breathlessness worsens
pearls:
- Exacerbation: bronchodilators, prednisolone 40 mg × 5 days, antibiotics if purulent sputum (amoxicillin/doxycycline), controlled oxygen (target SpO2 88–92%)
- High-flow oxygen can cause CO2 retention in COPD
danger:
- Confusion, drowsiness, cyanosis, severe breathlessness → hospital
drugs: salbutamol, ipratropium, beclometasone, budesonide-formoterol, prednisolone, amoxicillin, doxycycline, aminophylline

aka: CF; mucoviscidosis

=== disease: peptic-ulcer
name: Peptic Ulcer Disease
category: Gastrointestinal
stg: Ch. 10 (§10.2.1–10.2.3)
notes: Pulmonary_GI_Hepatic_Study_Notes#4
quickref: Peptic Ulcer Disease
aka: PUD; gastric ulcer; duodenal ulcer; H. pylori; vidonda vya tumbo
investigations:
- H. pylori stool antigen or urea breath test (off PPI for 2 weeks), serology (initial only)
- Endoscopy if alarm features (age >55 new symptoms, weight loss, dysphagia, anaemia, vomiting, GI bleeding)
- FBC for anaemia
advice:
- Complete the full 14-day H. pylori course even if pain stops early
- Take omeprazole 30–60 minutes before breakfast
- Avoid NSAIDs/aspirin (use paracetamol), smoking, alcohol
- Black stools or vomiting blood = emergency
pearls:
- H. pylori triple therapy: PPI + amoxicillin + clarithromycin (metronidazole if penicillin allergy) for 14 days
- NSAID ulcer: stop NSAID, PPI 8 weeks; if NSAID essential, add PPI
- Upper GI bleed: resuscitate, IV pantoprazole 80 mg then 8 mg/h, endoscopy
danger:
- Haematemesis, melaena, severe sudden abdominal pain (perforation) → emergency
drugs: omeprazole, amoxicillin, metronidazole, antacid

=== disease: gord
name: Gastro-oesophageal Reflux Disease (GORD/GERD)
category: Gastrointestinal
stg: Ch. 10
notes: Pulmonary_GI_Hepatic_Study_Notes#5
quickref: Gastroesophageal Reflux Disease
aka: GERD; GORD; heartburn; reflux; acid reflux
investigations:
- Clinical diagnosis; trial of PPI; endoscopy if alarm features or no response
advice:
- Raise the head of the bed, avoid lying down within 3 hours of eating, smaller meals
- Avoid trigger foods (fatty/spicy food, coffee, alcohol, chocolate), stop smoking, lose weight
- Antacids give quick relief; take PPI before breakfast
pearls:
- Drugs that worsen reflux: nitrates, CCBs, theophylline, anticholinergics, bisphosphonates, NSAIDs
- Pregnancy heartburn: lifestyle, antacids (alginate), then ranitidine-type/PPI
danger:
- Difficulty or pain on swallowing, weight loss, vomiting blood → endoscopy
drugs: omeprazole, antacid, metoclopramide

quickref: Inflammatory Bowel Disease (Ulcerative Colitis and Crohn's Disease)
aka: IBD; ulcerative colitis; UC; Crohn's disease
investigations:
- Colonoscopy with biopsy, stool culture (exclude infection incl. amoebiasis/TB), faecal calprotectin, FBC, CRP, albumin

=== disease: constipation-diarrhoea
name: Constipation and Diarrhoea
category: Gastrointestinal
stg: Ch. 10; Ch. 1 (§1.17 dehydration)
notes: Pulmonary_GI_Hepatic_Study_Notes#7
quickref: Constipation and Diarrhoea
aka: constipation; diarrhoea; loose stools; kuharisha; choo kigumu
investigations:
- Diarrhoea: assess dehydration; stool microscopy (amoeba, giardia), culture if bloody; malaria test if febrile
- Constipation: review drugs (opioids, iron, anticholinergics, CCBs), TFTs, calcium; alarm features → colonoscopy
advice:
- Diarrhoea: ORS after every loose stool, continue feeding/breastfeeding, zinc for 10 days in children, hand washing and safe water
- Constipation: fibre (vegetables, fruits, whole grains), 2 L water daily, exercise, do not ignore the urge
pearls:
- Antidiarrhoeals (loperamide) are not for children or bloody diarrhoea
- Opioid-induced constipation: stimulant + softener laxative; bulk laxatives are not suitable
danger:
- Signs of severe dehydration, blood in stool, high fever, persistent vomiting → refer
drugs: ors, zinc, loperamide, lactulose, bisacodyl, metronidazole, ciprofloxacin

=== disease: liver-disease
name: Liver Disease (Cirrhosis & Complications)
category: Gastrointestinal
stg: Ch. 10 (§10.4.1–10.4.3)
notes: Pulmonary_GI_Hepatic_Study_Notes#8
quickref: Liver Disease (Cirrhosis and Its Complications)
aka: cirrhosis; hepatitis B; hepatitis C; ascites; varices; hepatic encephalopathy; SBP; portal hypertension
investigations:
- LFTs, albumin, INR, FBC (platelets), HBsAg, anti-HCV, abdominal ultrasound, AFP (HCC screening), ascitic tap (SBP: neutrophils >250/mm³)
- Child-Pugh and MELD scoring
advice:
- No alcohol at all; avoid herbal remedies and unnecessary medicines
- Low-salt diet for ascites; weigh daily on diuretics
- Lactulose 2–3 soft stools daily to prevent confusion; report drowsiness or confusion
- Avoid NSAIDs and sedatives; paracetamol max 2 g/day
- Hepatitis B: vaccination of household contacts; do not stop tenofovir abruptly
pearls:
- Ascites: spironolactone 100 mg + furosemide 40 mg (ratio 100:40)
- Variceal bleed: octreotide/terlipressin, antibiotic prophylaxis (ceftriaxone), band ligation; propranolol for prophylaxis
- Hepatic encephalopathy: lactulose ± rifaximin; treat precipitants (bleeding, infection, constipation, sedatives)
danger:
- Vomiting blood, black stools, confusion, fever with abdominal pain → emergency
drugs: spironolactone, furosemide, lactulose, propranolol, ceftriaxone, ciprofloxacin, tld, vitamin-k

=== disease: pancreatitis
name: Pancreatitis (Acute & Chronic)
category: Gastrointestinal
stg: Ch. 10 (§10.4)
notes: Pulmonary_GI_Hepatic_Study_Notes#9
quickref: Pancreatitis
aka: acute pancreatitis; chronic pancreatitis
investigations:
- Serum lipase/amylase (>3× upper limit), ultrasound (gallstones), CT if diagnosis unclear or after 72 h for complications
- FBC, CRP, glucose, calcium, triglycerides, LFTs, creatinine
advice:
- Stop alcohol completely; small low-fat meals; pancreatic enzymes with food in chronic pancreatitis
pearls:
- Main causes: gallstones and alcohol ("I GET SMASHED"); drugs include azathioprine, valproate, thiazides, didanosine
- Aggressive IV fluids (Ringer's lactate), analgesia, early feeding; antibiotics only for infected necrosis
danger:
- Severe abdominal pain radiating to the back with vomiting and shock → emergency
drugs: morphine, paracetamol, metoclopramide, omeprazole

=== disease: diabetes
name: Diabetes Mellitus (incl. DKA/HHS & Hypoglycaemia)
category: Endocrine
stg: Ch. 19 (§19.1–19.11)
notes: Endocrine_Musculoskeletal_Neurological_Study_Notes#1
quickref: Diabetes Mellitus (Type 1, Type 2, DKA/HHS)
aka: DM; sugar; kisukari; type 1 diabetes; type 2 diabetes; DKA; HHS; hypoglycaemia
investigations:
- Diagnosis: fasting plasma glucose ≥7.0 mmol/L, 2-h OGTT ≥11.1, random ≥11.1 with symptoms, HbA1c ≥6.5%
- Monitoring: HbA1c 3–6 monthly, urine albumin:creatinine, creatinine/eGFR, lipids, BP, annual eye and foot examination
- DKA: glucose, ketones, blood gas (pH, bicarbonate), electrolytes (K+), creatinine, precipitants (infection, MI)
advice:
- Diet: regular meals, reduce sugar and refined starch (white bread, soda), more vegetables, portion control (ugali/rice)
- Exercise 150 minutes/week; stop smoking
- Recognise and treat hypoglycaemia (3 sugar cubes or ½ glass juice, recheck in 15 min, then a snack)
- Insulin storage: fridge or cool clay pot, never freeze; rotate injection sites
- Daily foot check; well-fitting shoes; never walk barefoot
- Sick-day rules: never stop insulin; pause metformin if vomiting/dehydrated; seek help early
pearls:
- Metformin is first line for type 2; stop if eGFR <30, hold for contrast
- DKA: fluids first → insulin 0.1 unit/kg/h → potassium (do not start insulin if K+ <3.3); add dextrose when glucose ≈ 14 mmol/L
- Sulfonylureas + elderly/renal impairment = prolonged hypoglycaemia (prefer gliclazide)
- Dolutegravir doubles metformin levels (max 1 g/day)
danger:
- Vomiting, abdominal pain, deep breathing, drowsiness (DKA); confusion/sweating/fits (hypoglycaemia) → emergency
drugs: metformin, glibenclamide, gliclazide, glimepiride, insulin-soluble, insulin-nph, dextrose-50, enalapril, losartan, atorvastatin, aspirin, amitriptyline

=== disease: osteoarthritis
name: Osteoarthritis
category: Musculoskeletal
stg: Ch. 17 (§17.2.4)
notes: Endocrine_Musculoskeletal_Neurological_Study_Notes#2
quickref: Osteoarthritis
aka: OA; degenerative joint disease; arthritis of the knee
investigations:
- Clinical diagnosis (age >45, activity-related pain, morning stiffness <30 min); X-ray (joint space narrowing, osteophytes)
- ESR/CRP and uric acid if inflammatory or gout suspected
advice:
- Weight loss and quadriceps-strengthening exercise are the most effective treatments
- Paracetamol regularly or topical NSAID gel; oral NSAIDs with food for short periods only
- Walking aids, supportive footwear
pearls:
- Topical NSAIDs preferred in elderly; oral NSAID + PPI if needed and no contraindication
- Intra-articular steroid for flares (max 3–4 per year)
danger:
- Hot, swollen joint with fever → septic arthritis (urgent)
drugs: paracetamol, ibuprofen, diclofenac, tramadol, prednisolone

=== disease: rheumatoid-arthritis
name: Rheumatoid Arthritis
category: Musculoskeletal
stg: Ch. 17 (§17.2.1)
notes: Endocrine_Musculoskeletal_Neurological_Study_Notes#3
quickref: Rheumatoid Arthritis
aka: RA; rheumatism; inflammatory arthritis
investigations:
- RF and anti-CCP, ESR/CRP, FBC, LFTs and creatinine (before DMARDs), X-rays of hands/feet (erosions), hepatitis B/C, chest X-ray
advice:
- Methotrexate is taken ONCE A WEEK only; folic acid on a different day
- Report sore throat, fever, mouth ulcers, cough; attend blood monitoring
- Use effective contraception on methotrexate; avoid alcohol
- Joint protection, exercise, stop smoking
pearls:
- Start DMARD (methotrexate) early — within 3 months of symptoms; steroids as bridge only
- Methotrexate + co-trimoxazole = fatal marrow suppression
danger:
- Fever, sore throat or mouth ulcers on DMARDs → urgent FBC (neutropenia)
drugs: methotrexate, prednisolone, ibuprofen, diclofenac, folic-acid, paracetamol

=== disease: gout

=== disease: aki
name: Acute Kidney Injury
category: Renal & Urology
stg: Ch. 21 (§21.2)

quickref: Acute Kidney Injury
aka: AKI; acute renal failure
investigations:
- Serum creatinine (rise ≥26.5 µmol/L in 48 h or ≥1.5× baseline), urine output (<0.5 mL/kg/h for 6 h)

=== disease: poisoning
name: Common Poisons & General Management of Poisoning
category: Toxicology & Poisoning
stg: Ch. 25 (§25.1–25.6)
notes: Hematological_Cancer_Toxicology_Study_Notes#18
quickref: Organophosphate and Carbamate Poisoning
aka: poisoning; overdose; organophosphate; paracetamol overdose; kerosene; iron poisoning; aspirin poisoning; antidotes; snake bite; sumu
