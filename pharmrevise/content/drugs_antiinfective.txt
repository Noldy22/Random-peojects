# Drug monographs — anti-infectives. AWaRe group shown in the class line (NEMLIT 2021 adopts WHO AWaRe).

=== drug: amoxicillin
name: Amoxicillin
class: Aminopenicillin (beta-lactam) — AWaRe ACCESS
group: Anti-infective
nemlit: A (capsule 250 mg; dispersible tablet 125, 250 mg; suspension)
forms: Capsule 250 mg, 500 mg; dispersible tablet 125 mg, 250 mg; suspension 125 mg/5 mL, 250 mg/5 mL
moa: Binds penicillin-binding proteins → inhibits bacterial cell-wall cross-linking → bactericidal; destroyed by β-lactamase.
uses:
- Pneumonia in children (dispersible tablets — first-line) and community-acquired pneumonia in adults
- Acute otitis media, sinusitis, streptococcal pharyngotonsillitis
- H. pylori eradication; UTI (if sensitive); dental infections; enterococcal infection; prophylaxis of endocarditis
dose:
- Adults: 500 mg every 8 hours (1 g every 8 hours for pneumonia), 5–7 days
- Children: 40–50 mg/kg/dose twice daily (pneumonia, otitis) for 5 days — STG paediatric pneumonia uses 40 mg/kg BD
- H. pylori: 1 g twice daily for 14 days (with PPI + clarithromycin)
ci:
- Penicillin allergy (anaphylaxis history)
- Infectious mononucleosis (very high rate of maculopapular rash)
se:
- Diarrhoea, nausea, rash (maculopapular), candidiasis
- Anaphylaxis (rare), antibiotic-associated colitis
ddi:
- Methotrexate :: Reduced methotrexate excretion → toxicity :: Monitor
- Warfarin :: May increase INR :: Monitor INR
- Allopurinol :: Increased rash risk :: Monitor
- Combined oral contraceptives :: Clinically insignificant (no extra precaution needed per current guidance) :: Counsel if vomiting/diarrhoea
food:
- Food :: Does not affect absorption :: Take with or without food
preg: Safe.
lact: Compatible.
renal: Reduce frequency if eGFR <30.
monitor:
- Clinical response in 48–72 h; rash
advice:
- Complete the full course even if you feel better
- Shake suspension well; keep reconstituted suspension in a cool place and discard after 7–14 days (check label)
- Stop and seek help immediately if you develop swelling, wheeze or widespread rash
storage: Dry powder below 25°C; reconstituted suspension in fridge (or cool place), discard after 7–14 days.
pearls:
- Always ask about penicillin allergy before dispensing any beta-lactam

=== drug: amoxicillin-clavulanate
name: Amoxicillin + clavulanic acid (co-amoxiclav)
class: Aminopenicillin + beta-lactamase inhibitor — AWaRe ACCESS
group: Anti-infective
nemlit: B/C (tablet 500/125 mg; suspension 125/31.25, 250/62.5 per 5 mL)
forms: Tablet 375 mg (250/125), 625 mg (500/125), 1 g (875/125); suspension; injection 1.2 g
moa: Clavulanate irreversibly inhibits many β-lactamases, extending amoxicillin's spectrum to β-lactamase producers (S. aureus, H. influenzae, M. catarrhalis, anaerobes).
uses:
- Animal and human bites, cellulitis, diabetic foot infection (mild)
- Sinusitis/otitis media failing amoxicillin, aspiration pneumonia, dental abscess, UTI (resistant)
dose:
- Adults: 625 mg every 8 hours or 1 g every 12 hours for 5–7 days
- Children: 25–45 mg/kg/day (amoxicillin component) in 2–3 doses
ci:
- Penicillin allergy; previous cholestatic jaundice with co-amoxiclav
se:
- Diarrhoea (more than amoxicillin), nausea, candidiasis, rash
- Cholestatic hepatitis (especially elderly men, long courses)
ddi:
- Warfarin :: Raised INR :: Monitor
- Methotrexate :: Toxicity :: Monitor
food:
- Food :: Improves clavulanate absorption and reduces GI upset :: Take at the start of a meal
preg: Generally safe (avoid in preterm rupture of membranes — NEC risk).
lact: Compatible.
hepatic: Monitor LFTs.
monitor:
- LFTs if prolonged therapy
advice:
- Take at the start of a meal; complete the course
- Report yellow skin/eyes or dark urine
storage: Below 25°C; suspension refrigerated, discard after 7 days.
pearls:
- Diarrhoea is mainly from the clavulanate component

=== drug: benzylpenicillin
name: Benzylpenicillin (penicillin G, crystalline penicillin)
class: Natural penicillin (beta-lactam) — AWaRe ACCESS
group: Anti-infective
nemlit: A/B (powder for injection 600 mg = 1 MU, 3 g = 5 MU)
forms: Powder for injection 600 mg (1 million units), 3 g (5 million units)
moa: Cell-wall synthesis inhibitor; excellent against streptococci, pneumococci (sensitive), meningococci, Treponema, Clostridium.
uses:
- Severe pneumonia in children (with gentamicin), neonatal sepsis (with gentamicin)
- Meningococcal disease (pre-referral), tetanus, neurosyphilis, streptococcal cellulitis/erysipelas, endocarditis
dose:
- Adults: 1.2–2.4 g (2–4 MU) IV/IM every 4–6 hours (meningitis up to 2.4 g every 4 h)
- Children: 50,000 IU/kg (30 mg/kg) IV/IM every 6 hours (neonates every 12 h in week 1)
ci:
- Penicillin hypersensitivity
se:
- Hypersensitivity (urticaria, anaphylaxis), pain at injection site
- High doses: seizures (especially renal failure), electrolyte disturbance; Jarisch–Herxheimer reaction in syphilis
ddi:
- Probenecid :: Raises penicillin levels (used deliberately) :: —
- Methotrexate :: Toxicity :: Monitor
- Tetracyclines / chloramphenicol :: Bacteriostatic drugs may antagonise penicillin :: Avoid combination in meningitis
food:
- Not applicable (injection)
preg: Safe.
lact: Compatible.
renal: Reduce dose in severe renal impairment (seizure risk).
monitor:
- Clinical response; signs of hypersensitivity for 30 min after first dose
advice:
- Inform staff about any previous penicillin reaction
storage: Powder below 25°C; use reconstituted solution immediately.
pearls:
- Have adrenaline ready whenever giving parenteral penicillin

=== drug: benzathine-penicillin
name: Benzathine benzylpenicillin
class: Long-acting depot penicillin — AWaRe ACCESS
group: Anti-infective
nemlit: A/B (powder for injection 1.44 g = 2.4 MU)
forms: Powder for injection 1.44 g (2.4 million units)
moa: Slowly hydrolysed to benzylpenicillin → low, prolonged (2–4 week) penicillin levels.
uses:
- Syphilis (primary, secondary, early latent; late latent weekly × 3); genital ulcer syndrome (syndromic)
- Secondary prophylaxis of rheumatic fever; streptococcal pharyngitis (single dose)
dose:
- Early syphilis/GUD: 2.4 MU deep IM single dose (1.2 MU into each buttock)
- Late latent syphilis: 2.4 MU IM weekly for 3 weeks
- Rheumatic fever prophylaxis: adults 1.2 MU IM every 3–4 weeks (children <30 kg: 600,000 IU)
ci:
- Penicillin allergy; NEVER give intravenously (cardiac arrest, death)
se:
- Painful injection, sterile abscess; Jarisch–Herxheimer reaction (fever, chills within 24 h in syphilis); anaphylaxis
ddi:
- Tetracyclines :: Possible antagonism :: Avoid combining
food:
- Not applicable
preg: Safe — the ONLY effective treatment for syphilis in pregnancy (prevents congenital syphilis).
lact: Compatible.
monitor:
- Observe for 30 minutes after injection; syphilis serology (RPR) at 3, 6, 12 months
advice:
- Abstain from sex until you and your partner(s) are treated; partners must be treated too
- Fever and aches within a day of the injection may occur (Jarisch–Herxheimer) — not an allergy; take paracetamol
storage: Below 25°C.
pearls:
- Deep IM only, upper outer quadrant of buttock; aspirate before injecting

=== drug: phenoxymethylpenicillin

- Penicillin allergy

- Diarrhoea, nausea, rash, hypersensitivity
ddi:
- Methotrexate :: Toxicity :: Monitor
food:
- Food :: Reduces absorption :: Take 1 hour before or 2 hours after food
preg: Safe.
lact: Compatible.
monitor:
- Adherence (prophylaxis)
advice:
- Take on an empty stomach; full 10 days for sore throat (prevents rheumatic fever)
- Sickle cell: give every day without missing; seek care urgently for any fever
storage: Suspension refrigerated, discard after 7 days.
pearls:
- 10-day course is needed to eradicate Group A Strep and prevent rheumatic fever

=== drug: cloxacillin

=== drug: gentamicin
name: Gentamicin
class: Aminoglycoside — AWaRe ACCESS
group: Anti-infective
nemlit: A/B (injection 40 mg/mL, 20 mg/mL in 2 mL)
forms: Injection 40 mg/mL (2 mL), 10 mg/mL paediatric; eye/ear drops 0.3%
moa: Binds 30S ribosomal subunit → misreading of mRNA → bactericidal (concentration-dependent, with post-antibiotic effect); needs oxygen for uptake (inactive against anaerobes).
uses:
- Neonatal sepsis and severe pneumonia in children (with ampicillin/benzylpenicillin)
- Pyelonephritis, intra-abdominal and pelvic sepsis (with ampicillin/metronidazole), endocarditis (synergy)
- Gonorrhoea (single 240 mg IM dose with azithromycin, alternative)
dose:
- Adults: 5–7 mg/kg IV/IM once daily
- Children: 7.5 mg/kg once daily; neonates 5 mg/kg once daily (every 36–48 h if preterm/low birth weight)
ci:
- Myasthenia gravis; previous aminoglycoside ototoxicity; pregnancy (avoid)
se:
- Nephrotoxicity (usually reversible), ototoxicity (vestibular and cochlear — irreversible)
- Neuromuscular blockade (high doses, with anaesthetics or MgSO4)
ddi:
- Furosemide :: Additive ototoxicity :: Avoid or monitor
- Vancomycin / amphotericin B / cisplatin / NSAIDs :: Additive nephrotoxicity :: Monitor creatinine and levels
- Neuromuscular blockers / MgSO4 :: Enhanced neuromuscular blockade :: Caution
food:
- Not applicable
preg: Avoid — risk of fetal eighth-nerve damage (use only if life-threatening).
lact: Compatible (poor oral absorption by infant).
renal: Extend interval and reduce dose; monitor levels.
monitor:
- Creatinine before and every 2–3 days; trough levels (<1 mg/L for once-daily dosing); hearing/balance
advice:
- Report ringing in ears, hearing loss, dizziness or reduced urine
storage: Below 25°C.
pearls:
- Limit courses to ≤7 days where possible; once-daily dosing is as effective and less toxic

=== drug: ceftriaxone
name: Ceftriaxone
class: Third-generation cephalosporin — AWaRe WATCH
group: Anti-infective
nemlit: B (injection 250 mg, 500 mg, 1 g) — level A for STI only
forms: Powder for injection 250 mg, 500 mg, 1 g
moa: β-lactam cell-wall inhibitor; broad Gram-negative cover, good CNS penetration; long half-life allows once-daily dosing.
uses:
- Severe pneumonia, bacterial meningitis, sepsis, severe malaria with suspected bacterial co-infection
- Gonorrhoea/urethral and vaginal discharge (STI syndromic), PID, acute pyelonephritis, typhoid fever (severe), SBP
dose:
- Adults: 1–2 g IV/IM once daily; meningitis 2 g every 12 hours
- Gonorrhoea: 500 mg IM single dose (STG 2021 lists 250 mg IM with azithromycin 1 g — check current guideline)
- Children: 50–80 mg/kg once daily; meningitis 100 mg/kg/day (max 4 g)
ci:
- Severe penicillin allergy (anaphylaxis) — cross-reactivity low but caution
- Neonates with hyperbilirubinaemia; neonates receiving IV calcium (fatal precipitates)
se:
- Diarrhoea, C. difficile, rash, injection-site pain
- Biliary sludge/pseudolithiasis, raised LFTs; haemolytic anaemia (rare)
ddi:
- Calcium-containing IV fluids (Ringer's lactate) :: Ceftriaxone–calcium precipitates in lungs/kidneys :: Never mix or co-infuse; flush line; avoid in neonates
- Warfarin :: Raised INR :: Monitor
food:
- Not applicable
preg: Safe.
lact: Compatible.
renal: No adjustment usually needed (dual elimination).
monitor:
- Response at 48–72 h; switch to oral when improving (IV-to-oral switch)
advice:
- IM injection is painful — usually reconstituted with 1% lidocaine for IM use (never give lidocaine-reconstituted drug IV)
storage: Powder below 25°C; use reconstituted solution promptly.
pearls:
- WATCH antibiotic: reserve for indications in STG; misuse drives ESBL resistance

=== drug: ciprofloxacin
name: Ciprofloxacin
class: Fluoroquinolone — AWaRe WATCH
group: Anti-infective
nemlit: A (tablet 250, 500 mg); C (IV)
forms: Tablet 250 mg, 500 mg; IV 2 mg/mL; eye/ear drops 0.3%
moa: Inhibits bacterial DNA gyrase (topoisomerase II) and topoisomerase IV → bactericidal; strong Gram-negative and Pseudomonas activity.
uses:
- Dysentery (shigellosis), typhoid (if sensitive), cholera (adults — single dose)
- Complicated UTI/pyelonephritis, prostatitis; SBP prophylaxis
- Anthrax and plague; bone and joint infections (Gram-negative)
dose:
- Adults: 500 mg every 12 hours (250–750 mg); UTI 3 days (uncomplicated) to 7–14 days
- Shigellosis: adults 500 mg twice daily for 3 days; children 15 mg/kg twice daily for 3 days
- Cholera: adults 1 g single dose
ci:
- History of tendon disorder with quinolones; myasthenia gravis
- Children and adolescents (relative — use only when benefit clear, e.g. shigellosis); pregnancy (relative)
se:
- GI upset, dizziness, headache, photosensitivity
- Tendinitis/tendon rupture (elderly, steroids), QT prolongation, seizures, C. difficile, peripheral neuropathy, dysglycaemia
ddi:
- Antacids / iron / zinc / calcium / dairy :: Chelation → large fall in absorption :: Take ciprofloxacin 2 h before or 6 h after
- Theophylline / aminophylline :: Theophylline toxicity (seizures) :: Avoid or reduce dose
- Warfarin :: Raised INR :: Monitor
- NSAIDs :: Increased seizure risk :: Caution
- Corticosteroids :: Tendon rupture :: Avoid combination in elderly
- QT drugs (amiodarone, haloperidol) :: Arrhythmia :: Avoid
food:
- Milk, yoghurt, calcium-fortified juice (alone) :: Reduced absorption :: Do not take with dairy alone; normal meals with dairy are acceptable
- Caffeine :: Ciprofloxacin raises caffeine levels → jitteriness :: Limit coffee
preg: Avoid (use only if no alternative).
lact: Avoid.
renal: Halve dose if eGFR <30.
monitor:
- Response; tendon pain; glucose in diabetics
advice:
- Do not take with milk, antacids, iron or zinc — separate by at least 2 hours before / 6 hours after
- Stop and rest if you get pain or swelling in your heel or a tendon
- Avoid strong sunlight; drink plenty of water
storage: Below 25°C.
pearls:
- Ciprofloxacin is a WATCH antibiotic — avoid for simple respiratory infections (poor pneumococcal cover)

=== drug: doxycycline
name: Doxycycline
class: Tetracycline — AWaRe ACCESS
group: Anti-infective
nemlit: A (capsule 100 mg)
forms: Capsule/tablet 100 mg
moa: Binds 30S ribosomal subunit → inhibits protein synthesis (bacteriostatic); active against atypicals, Chlamydia, Rickettsia, Borrelia, cholera.
uses:
- Chlamydia, urethral/vaginal discharge and PID (syndromic regimens), lymphogranuloma venereum
- Atypical pneumonia, COPD exacerbation, cholera (single dose), brucellosis, tick-borne relapsing fever, rickettsial fever, leptospirosis
- Malaria prophylaxis for travellers; acne
dose:
- Adults: 100 mg twice daily (200 mg on day 1 in some regimens) for 7 days (chlamydia/urethritis), 14 days (PID)
- Cholera: 300 mg single dose (adults)
ci:
- Pregnancy, children <8 years (tooth discolouration, bone growth), breastfeeding (prolonged)
se:
- Oesophageal ulceration (if taken lying down/without water), nausea, photosensitivity
- Candidiasis; benign intracranial hypertension (rare)
ddi:
- Antacids / iron / zinc / calcium / magnesium :: Chelation → reduced absorption :: Separate by 2–3 hours
- Warfarin :: Raised INR :: Monitor
- Carbamazepine / phenytoin / rifampicin :: Reduced doxycycline levels :: Consider higher dose
food:
- Milk/dairy :: Reduces absorption (less than older tetracyclines) :: Avoid taking with milk
- Food :: Reduces GI upset :: Can take with food
preg: CONTRAINDICATED (fetal tooth discolouration, bone effects; maternal hepatotoxicity).
lact: Avoid long courses.
monitor:
- Response
advice:
- Swallow with a full glass of water while sitting or standing; do not lie down for 30 minutes
- Avoid strong sunlight; use sunscreen
- Separate from milk, antacids and iron tablets
- For STIs: avoid sex until you and partners complete treatment
storage: Below 25°C, protect from light; discard expired tetracyclines (degradation products are nephrotoxic).
pearls:
- Pill oesophagitis is a classic counselling point — water + upright

=== drug: azithromycin
name: Azithromycin
class: Macrolide — AWaRe WATCH
group: Anti-infective
nemlit: B (capsule/tablet 250, 500 mg; liquid 200 mg/5 mL) — level A for STI only
forms: Tablet/capsule 250 mg, 500 mg; suspension 200 mg/5 mL
moa: Binds 50S ribosomal subunit → inhibits protein synthesis; long tissue half-life allows short courses.
uses:
- Chlamydia and gonorrhoea (dual therapy), trachoma (MDA), chancroid
- Atypical/community-acquired pneumonia (penicillin allergy), typhoid fever (uncomplicated), cholera in children/pregnancy, pertussis
dose:
- Chlamydia/urethritis: 1 g single dose
- Typhoid: 20 mg/kg (max 1 g) once daily for 7 days
- Respiratory: 500 mg once daily for 3 days (children 10 mg/kg once daily × 3 days)
- Trachoma MDA: 20 mg/kg single dose (max 1 g)
ci:
- Macrolide allergy; hepatic disease (caution)
se:
- GI upset (diarrhoea, nausea, abdominal pain), QT prolongation, hepatotoxicity (rare), hearing loss (high doses)
ddi:
- QT-prolonging drugs (amiodarone, haloperidol, quinine, ondansetron) :: Torsades risk :: Avoid/monitor
- Antacids (Al/Mg) :: Reduced peak levels :: Separate by 2 hours
- Warfarin :: Raised INR :: Monitor
- Digoxin :: Raised digoxin :: Monitor
food:
- Food :: Capsules: take 1 h before or 2 h after food; tablets/suspension: with or without food
preg: Safe (used for chlamydia and cholera in pregnancy).
lact: Compatible.
hepatic: Avoid in severe hepatic impairment.
monitor:
- Response; ECG if cardiac risk
advice:
- Even a 1-day or 3-day course keeps working for days — complete the dose as instructed
- STIs: partner notification and treatment; no sex for 7 days after treatment
storage: Below 30°C; reconstituted suspension 5–10 days.
pearls:
- Weaker CYP3A4 inhibitor than clarithromycin/erythromycin — fewer interactions

=== drug: erythromycin
name: Erythromycin
class: Macrolide — AWaRe WATCH
group: Anti-infective
nemlit: A (tablet 250 mg; suspension 125 mg/5 mL)

hepatic: Caution.

=== drug: cotrimoxazole
name: Co-trimoxazole (sulfamethoxazole + trimethoprim)
class: Sulfonamide + dihydrofolate reductase inhibitor — AWaRe ACCESS
group: Anti-infective
nemlit: A (tablet 480 mg [400/80], 960 mg; suspension 240 mg/5 mL)
forms: Tablet 480 mg, 960 mg; paediatric tablet 120 mg; suspension 240 mg/5 mL
moa: Sequential blockade of folate synthesis (dihydropteroate synthase and dihydrofolate reductase) → bactericidal; also active against Pneumocystis, Toxoplasma, Isospora.
uses:
- Opportunistic infection prophylaxis in HIV (PLHIV with CD4 ≤350 or WHO stage 3/4; HIV-exposed infants from 6 weeks)
- Pneumocystis pneumonia (high-dose treatment), toxoplasmosis
- UTI (if sensitive), shigellosis (if sensitive), mycetoma (actinomycetoma)
dose:
- Prophylaxis: adults 960 mg once daily; children by weight (e.g. 6 weeks–6 months 120 mg; 6 months–5 years 240 mg; 6–14 years 480 mg daily)
- PCP treatment: 15–20 mg/kg/day trimethoprim component in 3–4 divided doses for 21 days (with prednisolone if hypoxic)
- UTI: 960 mg twice daily for 3–7 days
ci:
- Sulfonamide allergy; severe hepatic or renal impairment; neonates <6 weeks (kernicterus)
- First trimester (folate antagonism) and late pregnancy (kernicterus) — though prophylaxis continues in PLHIV
- G6PD deficiency (haemolysis)
se:
- Rash (common), Stevens–Johnson syndrome/TEN (stop at first sign of rash)
- Nausea, bone marrow suppression (folate deficiency), hyperkalaemia (trimethoprim), raised creatinine, hepatitis
ddi:
- Warfarin :: Marked INR rise :: Avoid or reduce warfarin, monitor INR closely
- Methotrexate :: Severe bone marrow suppression :: Avoid
- Phenytoin :: Raised phenytoin levels :: Monitor
- ACE inhibitors / spironolactone :: Hyperkalaemia :: Monitor K+
- Sulfonylureas :: Hypoglycaemia :: Monitor glucose
- Zidovudine :: Additive marrow toxicity :: Monitor FBC
food:
- Fluids :: Prevent crystalluria :: Drink plenty of water
preg: PLHIV continue CPT in pregnancy (benefit > risk) — but they then do not need SP for IPTp.
lact: Compatible (avoid in premature, jaundiced, G6PD-deficient infants).
renal: Halve dose if eGFR 15–30; avoid below 15.
monitor:
- Rash; FBC with long-term or high-dose use; K+
advice:
- Take every day with plenty of water
- STOP and seek help immediately if you get a skin rash, blisters, mouth sores or eye redness
- Women on CPT in pregnancy do not take SP (Fansidar) for malaria prevention
storage: Below 25°C, protect from light.
pearls:
- CPT + SP together increases sulfa toxicity — classic exam point

=== drug: nitrofurantoin
name: Nitrofurantoin
class: Nitrofuran urinary antiseptic — AWaRe ACCESS
group: Anti-infective
nemlit: A (tablet 100 mg)
forms: Tablet 50 mg, 100 mg; modified-release capsule 100 mg
moa: Reduced by bacterial flavoproteins to reactive intermediates that damage DNA/ribosomes; concentrated in urine only.
uses:
- Uncomplicated lower UTI (cystitis), including in pregnancy (avoid at term)
