# Drug monographs — analgesics, neurology, psychiatry.

=== drug: paracetamol
name: Paracetamol (acetaminophen)
class: Non-opioid analgesic and antipyretic
group: Analgesic
nemlit: A (tablet 500 mg; syrup 125 mg/5 mL; suppository 125 mg); D (IV 10 mg/mL)
forms: Tablet 500 mg; syrup 120–125 mg/5 mL; suppository 125 mg, 250 mg; IV infusion 10 mg/mL
moa: Central COX inhibition (weak peripheral anti-inflammatory effect); acts on hypothalamic heat-regulating centre.
uses:
- Mild–moderate pain (step 1 of WHO ladder, and with opioids at steps 2–3)
- Fever (malaria, infections); headache, dysmenorrhoea, osteoarthritis
dose:
- Adults: 500 mg–1 g every 4–6 hours (max 4 g/24 h); STG emergency: 1 g every 6–8 hours
- Children: 15 mg/kg every 6 hours when required (max 4 doses/24 h, max 60 mg/kg/day)
- Reduce to max 2–3 g/day in chronic alcohol use, low body weight (<50 kg), malnutrition, liver disease
ci:
- Severe hepatic impairment; hypersensitivity
se:
- Rare at therapeutic doses: rash, blood dyscrasias
- Overdose: hepatotoxicity (may be delayed 24–72 h with few early symptoms)
ddi:
- Warfarin :: Regular use (>2 g/day for several days) raises INR :: Monitor INR
- Enzyme inducers (rifampicin, carbamazepine, phenytoin, isoniazid) / chronic alcohol :: More toxic metabolite (NAPQI) :: Use lower maximum doses
- Metoclopramide :: Speeds paracetamol absorption :: Used intentionally in migraine
food:
- Alcohol (chronic/heavy) :: Increased liver toxicity :: Avoid alcohol; lower max dose
- Food :: Slightly slows onset :: Can be taken with or without food
preg: Analgesic of choice in pregnancy.
lact: Compatible.
hepatic: Reduce dose; avoid in severe disease.
monitor:
- Total daily dose from all products (many combination products contain paracetamol)
advice:
- Do not exceed 8 × 500 mg tablets in 24 hours; leave at least 4 hours between doses
- Check other medicines (cold/flu remedies) for hidden paracetamol
- Measure children's syrup with a syringe/spoon, by weight
- Overdose is dangerous even if you feel well — seek help immediately
storage: Below 25°C; keep out of reach of children.
pearls:
- Antidote: N-acetylcysteine (most effective within 8 hours of ingestion)

=== drug: ibuprofen
name: Ibuprofen
class: Non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID, propionic acid)
group: Analgesic
nemlit: A (tablet 200, 400 mg; syrup 100 mg/5 mL)
forms: Tablet 200 mg, 400 mg; suspension 100 mg/5 mL
moa: Reversible non-selective inhibition of COX-1 and COX-2 → ↓ prostaglandins (analgesic, antipyretic, anti-inflammatory).
uses:
- Mild–moderate pain, dysmenorrhoea, dental pain, musculoskeletal pain, headache/migraine, gout flare (acute)
- Fever in children (alternative to paracetamol); osteoarthritis, rheumatoid arthritis (anti-inflammatory)
dose:
- Adults: 200–400 mg every 6–8 hours with food (max 1.2 g/day OTC; 2.4 g/day prescribed)
- Children >3 months: 5–10 mg/kg every 6–8 hours (max 30 mg/kg/day)
ci:
- Active peptic ulcer/GI bleeding; history of NSAID-induced asthma or urticaria
- Severe heart failure, severe renal or hepatic impairment
- Third trimester of pregnancy; dengue/viral haemorrhagic fever (bleeding risk); haemophilia
se:
- Dyspepsia, peptic ulcer, GI bleeding
- Fluid retention, raised BP, AKI; bronchospasm in sensitive asthmatics; increased CV risk at high doses
ddi:
- ACE inhibitors / ARBs + diuretics :: "Triple whammy" — acute kidney injury; reduced BP control :: Avoid combination
- Warfarin / aspirin / clopidogrel / SSRIs :: Increased bleeding :: Avoid or use PPI
- Aspirin (low-dose cardioprotection) :: Ibuprofen blocks aspirin's antiplatelet effect :: Give aspirin 30 min before ibuprofen
- Lithium / methotrexate :: Reduced renal excretion → toxicity :: Avoid or monitor levels
- Corticosteroids :: GI ulceration :: Add PPI
food:
- Food or milk :: Reduces GI irritation :: Take with or after food
- Alcohol :: GI bleeding :: Avoid
preg: Avoid, especially after 20 weeks (oligohydramnios) and in the third trimester (premature closure of ductus arteriosus).
lact: Compatible (preferred NSAID).
renal: Avoid in CKD/AKI.
monitor:
- BP, renal function and GI symptoms with regular use
advice:
- Take with or after food; use the lowest dose for the shortest time
- Avoid if you have stomach ulcers, kidney problems or asthma triggered by painkillers
- Report black stools, stomach pain, swollen ankles
storage: Below 25°C.
pearls:
- Avoid NSAIDs in suspected dengue/VHF, heart failure, CKD and with ACEi + diuretic

=== drug: diclofenac
name: Diclofenac
class: NSAID (acetic acid derivative)
group: Analgesic
nemlit: A (injection 25 mg/mL; suppository 100 mg; gel); C (tablet 50, 100 mg)
forms: Tablet 25 mg, 50 mg (enteric-coated), 100 mg SR; injection 25 mg/mL (3 mL); suppository 50, 100 mg; gel 1%
moa: Non-selective COX inhibitor (relatively COX-2 preferential).
uses:
- Acute musculoskeletal pain, renal colic (IM/suppository), post-operative pain, gout flare, dysmenorrhoea
- Osteoarthritis and rheumatoid arthritis (topical gel for OA)
dose:
- Oral: 50 mg two to three times daily with food (max 150 mg/day)
- IM: 75 mg deep IM once (twice daily max, 2 days); renal colic 75 mg IM
- Suppository: 100 mg at night
ci:
- As ibuprofen; plus ischaemic heart disease, cerebrovascular disease, peripheral arterial disease, heart failure (NYHA II–IV)
se:
- GI ulceration/bleeding, raised LFTs, AKI, fluid retention, higher thrombotic CV risk than ibuprofen
- IM: injection-site necrosis/abscess; suppository: rectal irritation
ddi:
- ACE inhibitors / diuretics :: AKI ("triple whammy") :: Avoid
- Warfarin / anticoagulants :: Bleeding :: Avoid
- Methotrexate / lithium :: Toxicity :: Avoid
- Corticosteroids :: Ulceration :: PPI
food:
- Food :: Reduce GI upset :: Take with food; swallow enteric-coated tablets whole
- Alcohol :: GI bleeding :: Avoid
preg: Avoid (especially third trimester).
lact: Compatible short term.
renal: Avoid.
hepatic: Monitor LFTs with prolonged use.
monitor:
- BP, renal function, LFTs if long-term
advice:
- Swallow tablets whole with food; do not combine with other NSAIDs
- Gel: rub in gently 3–4 times daily, wash hands after
storage: Below 25°C.
pearls:
- Topical NSAIDs are preferred in elderly with knee/hand OA (fewer systemic effects)

=== drug: morphine
name: Morphine
class: Strong opioid agonist (μ-receptor)
group: Analgesic
nemlit: Listed (tablet 10 mg; oral liquid 10 mg/5 mL; SR granules; injection 10 mg/mL) — controlled drug
forms: Oral solution 10 mg/5 mL (immediate release); tablet 10 mg; SR tablets 10–100 mg; injection 10 mg/mL
moa: Agonist at μ (also κ, δ) opioid receptors in CNS and spinal cord → analgesia, euphoria, respiratory depression, miosis, reduced GI motility.
uses:
- Severe pain (step 3 of WHO ladder): cancer pain, sickle cell crisis, post-operative, MI pain
- Breathlessness in palliative care; acute pulmonary oedema (low dose, cautious)
dose:
- Opioid-naïve cancer pain: oral solution 2.5–5 mg every 4 hours + same dose as needed for breakthrough; titrate daily by 30–50%
- Convert to SR twice daily when stable (total daily dose ÷ 2); breakthrough dose = 1/6 of 24-h dose
- Acute severe pain: 0.1 mg/kg IV/SC every 2–4 hours (titrate in 1–2 mg increments IV)
ci:
- Acute respiratory depression, acute severe asthma (relative), raised ICP/head injury (unmonitored), paralytic ileus
- Concurrent MAOIs; acute alcoholism
se:
- Constipation (universal — always co-prescribe laxative), nausea/vomiting (first days), drowsiness, confusion
- Respiratory depression, hypotension, pruritus, urinary retention, miosis; tolerance and dependence
ddi:
- Benzodiazepines / alcohol / other CNS depressants :: Profound sedation, respiratory depression, death :: Avoid or reduce doses
- MAO inhibitors :: CNS excitation/depression :: Avoid
- Rifampicin :: Reduced morphine effect :: Monitor
food:
- Alcohol :: Fatal respiratory depression; SR "dose dumping" :: Avoid completely
preg: Use if needed; neonatal respiratory depression and withdrawal if used near delivery.
lact: Small amounts; monitor infant for sedation.
renal: Metabolites accumulate (M6G, M3G) — reduce dose and frequency.
hepatic: Reduce dose; may precipitate encephalopathy.
monitor:
- Pain score, respiratory rate, sedation score, bowel movements
advice:
- Take regularly "by the clock" for constant pain, plus extra (breakthrough) doses when needed
- Always take the prescribed laxative; drink plenty of fluids
- Drowsiness in first days usually improves; do not drive until you know the effects
- Keep locked away from children; return unused medicine to the pharmacy
storage: Controlled drug — locked cabinet, register every dose dispensed (Dangerous Drugs Register).
pearls:
- Opioid antidote: naloxone 0.4 mg IV, repeat every 2–3 min (short half-life — watch for re-sedation)
- Fear of addiction must not prevent adequate cancer pain relief

=== drug: tramadol
name: Tramadol
class: Weak opioid (μ agonist) with SNRI activity
group: Analgesic
nemlit: B (tablet 50 mg; injection 50 mg/mL)
forms: Capsule/tablet 50 mg; SR 100 mg; injection 50 mg/mL (2 mL)
moa: Weak μ-opioid agonist (via active metabolite O-desmethyltramadol, CYP2D6) and inhibitor of serotonin and noradrenaline reuptake.
uses:
- Moderate pain (step 2 of WHO ladder), post-operative pain, sickle cell crisis (moderate)
dose:
- 50–100 mg every 4–6 hours (max 400 mg/day; 300 mg in elderly >75)
ci:
- Uncontrolled epilepsy; MAOI use within 2 weeks; acute intoxication with CNS depressants
- Children <12 years (ultra-rapid metabolisers), breastfeeding (caution)
se:
- Nausea, dizziness, constipation, drowsiness, sweating, dry mouth
- Seizures (dose-related), serotonin syndrome, dependence and misuse
ddi:
- SSRIs / amitriptyline / MAOIs :: Serotonin syndrome and seizures :: Avoid or monitor closely
- Carbamazepine :: Reduced tramadol effect, seizure threshold lowered :: Avoid
- Benzodiazepines / alcohol :: Respiratory depression :: Avoid
- Warfarin :: Raised INR :: Monitor
- Ondansetron :: Reduced analgesia :: Monitor
food:
- Alcohol :: CNS depression :: Avoid
preg: Avoid unless needed (neonatal withdrawal).
lact: Avoid where possible.
renal: Extend interval to 12-hourly if eGFR <30.
monitor:
- Pain, sedation, bowel function, misuse
advice:
- May cause dizziness and nausea — avoid driving, alcohol
- Take a laxative if constipated
storage: Controlled substance in Tanzania — store securely.
pearls:
- Tramadol misuse is a growing public health problem — dispense only on valid prescription

=== drug: pethidine
name: Pethidine (meperidine)
class: Synthetic opioid agonist
group: Analgesic
nemlit: B (injection 50 mg/mL) — controlled drug
forms: Injection 50 mg/mL
moa: μ-opioid agonist; short duration (2–4 h); metabolite norpethidine is neurotoxic.
uses:
- Short-term acute pain, labour analgesia (widely used, but morphine/other preferred)
dose:
- 25–100 mg IM/SC every 4 hours (max 400 mg/day); labour 50–100 mg IM, repeat after 1–3 h (max 400 mg)
ci:
- Renal impairment (norpethidine accumulation → seizures); MAOIs (fatal serotonin reactions); sickle cell crisis (repeated use)
se:
- Nausea, vomiting, respiratory depression, tremor, seizures (norpethidine), neonatal respiratory depression
ddi:
- MAOIs / selegiline :: Serotonin syndrome, death :: Contraindicated
- SSRIs / tramadol :: Serotonin toxicity :: Avoid
- CNS depressants :: Respiratory depression :: Avoid
food:
- Alcohol :: CNS depression :: Avoid
preg: Labour analgesia; newborn may need naloxone.
lact: Avoid repeated doses.
renal: Avoid.
monitor:
- Respiratory rate, sedation; neonatal breathing if given in labour
advice:
- Hospital use
storage: Controlled drug register.
pearls:
- Not recommended for chronic or sickle-cell pain (neurotoxic metabolite)

=== drug: diazepam
name: Diazepam
class: Long-acting benzodiazepine
group: CNS
nemlit: A (injection 5 mg/mL); C (tablet 5 mg)
forms: Tablet 2 mg, 5 mg, 10 mg; injection 5 mg/mL (2 mL); rectal solution 5 mg, 10 mg
moa: Positive allosteric modulator of GABA-A receptors → ↑ frequency of chloride channel opening → anticonvulsant, anxiolytic, sedative, muscle relaxant.
uses:
- Status epilepticus and acute convulsions (first line), febrile convulsions
- Alcohol withdrawal (prevention of seizures and delirium tremens)
- Short-term anxiety, muscle spasm, tetanus spasms, eclampsia (where MgSO4 unavailable)
dose:
- Status epilepticus adults: 10 mg IV slowly (2–5 mg/min) or 10–20 mg rectally; repeat once after 5–10 min
- Children: 0.3 mg/kg IV (max 10 mg) or 0.5 mg/kg rectally (max 10–20 mg)
- Alcohol withdrawal: 10–20 mg orally every 1–2 hours as needed by symptoms (CIWA), then taper over days
- Anxiety: 2–5 mg two to three times daily for max 2–4 weeks
ci:
- Respiratory depression, severe COPD, sleep apnoea, myasthenia gravis, severe hepatic impairment
- Acute narrow-angle glaucoma; use as sole treatment of depression/chronic psychosis
se:
- Drowsiness, ataxia, confusion (elderly falls), respiratory depression (especially IV), hypotension
- Dependence and withdrawal (rebound anxiety, insomnia, seizures) with >2–4 weeks use; paradoxical agitation
ddi:
- Opioids / alcohol / other sedatives :: Respiratory depression, death :: Avoid; reduce doses
- Isoniazid / fluconazole / omeprazole / ritonavir :: Raised diazepam levels :: Reduce dose
- Rifampicin / carbamazepine :: Reduced diazepam levels :: Monitor
food:
- Alcohol :: Dangerous CNS depression :: Avoid completely
- Grapefruit juice :: Raises levels :: Avoid
preg: Avoid regular use (floppy infant, neonatal withdrawal); single doses for seizures acceptable.
lact: Avoid repeated doses (sedation in infant).
hepatic: Avoid in severe impairment (use lorazepam/oxazepam); can precipitate encephalopathy.
monitor:
- Respiratory rate, oxygen saturation, BP after IV doses; have bag-valve-mask ready
advice:
- Causes drowsiness — do not drive or operate machinery; no alcohol
- Use only for the short time prescribed; do not stop suddenly after long use
- Keep locked away (psychotropic — controlled)
storage: Controlled (psychotropic); IV solution absorbs into plastic — draw up just before use.
pearls:
- Antidote: flumazenil (use cautiously — can precipitate seizures in dependence/mixed overdose)
- Diazepam IM absorption is erratic — use IV or rectal

=== drug: phenobarbital
name: Phenobarbital (phenobarbitone)
class: Barbiturate anticonvulsant
group: CNS
nemlit: A (tablet 30 mg, 100 mg; injection 100 mg/2 mL)
forms: Tablet 30 mg, 100 mg; injection 200 mg/mL, 100 mg/2 mL; elixir
moa: Enhances GABA-A receptor activity (prolongs chloride channel opening); also reduces glutamate effects.
uses:
- Epilepsy (generalised tonic-clonic and focal) — cheap first-line option in resource-limited settings
- Status epilepticus (second line after benzodiazepine); neonatal seizures (drug of choice)
dose:
- Epilepsy adults: 60–180 mg at night (1–3 mg/kg); children 3–6 mg/kg/day
- Status epilepticus: 15–20 mg/kg IV at ≤50–100 mg/min (max 1 g)
- Neonatal seizures: 20 mg/kg IV/IM loading, then 2.5–5 mg/kg daily
ci:
- Acute porphyria; severe respiratory depression; severe hepatic impairment
se:
- Sedation, cognitive impairment, behavioural change/hyperactivity in children, depression
- Dependence; withdrawal seizures if stopped abruptly; rash, SJS; osteomalacia, folate deficiency
ddi:
- Hormonal contraceptives :: Strong enzyme induction → contraceptive failure :: Use DMPA/IUD
- Warfarin / steroids / ARVs (DTG, PIs, NNRTIs) / doxycycline :: Reduced levels :: Avoid or adjust; check ART interactions
- Valproate :: Raises phenobarbital levels :: Monitor sedation
- Alcohol / opioids / benzodiazepines :: Respiratory depression :: Avoid
food:
- Alcohol :: Additive CNS depression :: Avoid
preg: Teratogenic risk (cardiac, cleft) — but uncontrolled seizures are dangerous; give folic acid 5 mg; vitamin K to neonate.
lact: Sedation in infant — monitor.
hepatic: Reduce dose.
monitor:
- Seizure diary, sedation; levels 15–40 mg/L if available
advice:
- Take at night every day; never stop suddenly
- Women: tell your clinician before pregnancy; use effective contraception that is not affected
storage: Controlled; below 25°C.
pearls:
- Very long half-life (~100 h) — once-daily dosing; steady state after 2–3 weeks

=== drug: phenytoin
name: Phenytoin
class: Hydantoin anticonvulsant (sodium channel blocker)
group: CNS
nemlit: C (tablet/capsule 50, 100 mg; suspension 30 mg/5 mL; injection 100 mg/2 mL)
forms: Capsule 50 mg, 100 mg; suspension 30 mg/5 mL; injection 50 mg/mL
moa: Stabilises inactive state of voltage-gated sodium channels → limits repetitive neuronal firing.
uses:
- Status epilepticus (second line), generalised tonic-clonic and focal seizures
- Trigeminal neuralgia (second line)
dose:
- Status: 15–20 mg/kg IV in saline (not dextrose), max 50 mg/min (25 mg/min elderly/cardiac) with ECG monitoring
- Maintenance: 3–5 mg/kg/day (adults 200–400 mg daily), adjust by levels (10–20 mg/L)
ci:
- Sinus bradycardia, heart block, Adams–Stokes (IV); acute porphyria
se:
- Dose-related: nystagmus, ataxia, slurred speech, confusion (zero-order kinetics — small dose changes cause big level jumps)
- Gingival hyperplasia, hirsutism, coarse facies, acne; folate deficiency/megaloblastic anaemia; osteomalacia
- SJS/TEN (HLA-B*1502); IV: hypotension, arrhythmias, purple glove syndrome
ddi:
- Hormonal contraceptives / warfarin / steroids / ARVs :: Enzyme induction → reduced effect :: Use non-hormonal/DMPA contraception; adjust doses
- Isoniazid / fluconazole / co-trimoxazole / metronidazole / chloramphenicol :: Raised phenytoin → toxicity :: Monitor levels
- Valproate :: Displacement and inhibition :: Monitor free levels
- Antacids / enteral feeds :: Reduced absorption :: Separate by 2 h
food:
- Enteral feeds :: Markedly reduce absorption :: Stop feeds 2 h before and after dose
- Alcohol :: Acute intake raises, chronic lowers levels :: Avoid
preg: Fetal hydantoin syndrome — specialist review; folic acid 5 mg.
lact: Compatible.
hepatic: Reduce dose.
monitor:
- Plasma levels, FBC, LFTs, gums, folate
advice:
- Brush and floss carefully; regular dental checks (gum overgrowth)
- Do not switch brands; report rash, unsteadiness, double vision
storage: Below 25°C; IV solution crystallises in dextrose — use saline.
pearls:
- Non-linear (saturable) kinetics — classic pharmacokinetics exam topic

=== drug: carbamazepine
name: Carbamazepine
class: Anticonvulsant (sodium channel blocker); mood stabiliser
group: CNS
nemlit: A (tablet 100, 200 mg; syrup 100 mg/5 mL)
forms: Tablet 100 mg, 200 mg, 400 mg; CR tablets; syrup 100 mg/5 mL
moa: Blocks voltage-gated sodium channels; strong CYP3A4 inducer (including of itself — autoinduction).
uses:
- Focal (partial) seizures and generalised tonic-clonic seizures (not absence/myoclonic)
- Trigeminal neuralgia (first line), bipolar disorder, neuropathic pain
dose:
- Epilepsy adults: 100–200 mg once or twice daily, increase by 100–200 mg every 2 weeks; usual 400–1200 mg/day
- Children: 5 mg/kg/day, increase to 10–20 mg/kg/day in 2–3 doses
- Trigeminal neuralgia: 100 mg twice daily, increase to effect (usual 200 mg three to four times daily)
ci:
- AV conduction abnormalities, bone marrow depression, porphyria
- MAOIs; HLA-B*1502 (south-east Asians — SJS risk)
se:
- Dizziness, diplopia, ataxia, nausea (dose-related), hyponatraemia (SIADH)
- Rash (common), SJS/TEN, DRESS; leucopenia, aplastic anaemia (rare); hepatitis
ddi:
- Hormonal contraceptives :: Contraceptive failure :: Use DMPA, IUD
- Dolutegravir / ARVs (PIs, NNRTIs) :: Reduced ARV levels → failure :: Avoid; if essential DTG 50 mg twice daily
- Warfarin / steroids / doxycycline / haloperidol :: Reduced levels :: Adjust doses
- Erythromycin / clarithromycin / fluconazole / isoniazid :: Raised carbamazepine → toxicity :: Avoid or monitor levels
- Lithium :: Neurotoxicity :: Monitor
food:
- Grapefruit juice :: Raises carbamazepine levels :: Avoid
- Alcohol :: Increased CNS effects :: Avoid
preg: Neural tube defects (~1%) — folic acid 5 mg pre-conception; specialist review.
lact: Compatible.
hepatic: Caution.
monitor:
- FBC, sodium, LFTs at baseline and periodically; levels 4–12 mg/L
advice:
- Take with food; start low, increase slowly
- Report rash, fever, sore throat, mouth ulcers, unusual bruising, confusion
- Hormonal pills/implants will not work reliably
storage: Protect from moisture (tablets lose potency in humidity).
pearls:
- Autoinduction: dose needs to be increased after 2–4 weeks

=== drug: sodium-valproate
name: Sodium valproate
class: Broad-spectrum anticonvulsant; mood stabiliser
group: CNS
nemlit: C (tablet 200, 500 mg)
forms: Enteric-coated tablet 200 mg, 500 mg; CR tablets; syrup 200 mg/5 mL
moa: Increases GABA levels (inhibits GABA transaminase), blocks sodium and T-type calcium channels.
uses:
- Generalised epilepsies: tonic-clonic, absence, myoclonic (first line in men)
- Bipolar mania and prophylaxis; migraine prophylaxis
dose:
- Adults: 600 mg/day in 2 doses, increase by 200 mg every 3 days to 1–2 g/day (max 2.5 g)
- Children: 10–20 mg/kg/day, increase to 20–40 mg/kg/day in 2 doses
ci:
- Women and girls of childbearing potential unless no alternative and pregnancy prevention in place (teratogenic)
- Active liver disease; mitochondrial disorders; porphyria
se:
- Nausea, weight gain, tremor, hair loss (regrowth may be curly), drowsiness
- Hepatotoxicity (first 6 months, children <3 years), pancreatitis, thrombocytopenia, hyperammonaemic encephalopathy
- Teratogenicity: neural tube defects (1–2%), developmental delay (~30–40%)
ddi:
- Meropenem / carbapenems :: Valproate levels fall to sub-therapeutic within a day → seizures :: Avoid; use alternative antiepileptic
- Aspirin :: Displacement and increased free valproate :: Avoid
- Lamotrigine :: Valproate doubles lamotrigine levels → SJS risk :: Halve lamotrigine dose
- Carbamazepine / phenytoin / phenobarbital :: Mutual interactions :: Monitor levels
food:
- Food :: Reduces GI upset :: Take with or after food; swallow enteric-coated tablets whole
- Alcohol :: Additive sedation :: Avoid
preg: Avoid — highest teratogenic risk of the antiepileptics; specialist counselling; folic acid 5 mg.
lact: Compatible.
hepatic: Contraindicated in active liver disease.
monitor:
- LFTs and FBC (platelets) before starting and during the first 6 months; weight
advice:
- Women: valproate can seriously harm an unborn baby — use reliable contraception and plan pregnancy with your doctor
- Report vomiting, abdominal pain, yellow skin, unusual bruising, drowsiness
- Swallow tablets whole with food
storage: Keep in original foil (tablets absorb moisture).
pearls:
- Never prescribe to women of childbearing potential without a pregnancy prevention programme

=== drug: levetiracetam
name: Levetiracetam
class: Anticonvulsant (SV2A ligand)
group: CNS
nemlit: S (tablet 250–1000 mg; injection)
forms: Tablet 250 mg, 500 mg, 750 mg, 1000 mg; IV 100 mg/mL
moa: Binds synaptic vesicle protein 2A → modulates neurotransmitter release.
uses:
- Focal and generalised seizures; status epilepticus (second line IV); epilepsy in pregnancy (preferred with lamotrigine)
dose:
- 500 mg twice daily, increase by 500 mg BD every 2 weeks (max 1.5 g BD)
- Status epilepticus: 60 mg/kg IV (max 4.5 g) over 10 min
ci:
- Hypersensitivity
se:
- Somnolence, irritability, aggression, depression, suicidal ideation; dizziness
ddi:
- Few significant interactions (not hepatically metabolised)
food:
- None significant
preg: Among the safest antiepileptics in pregnancy.
lact: Compatible.
renal: Reduce dose according to eGFR.
monitor:
- Mood and behaviour
advice:
- Report mood changes or thoughts of self-harm
storage: Below 25°C.
pearls:
- Minimal interactions — useful with ART and TB drugs

=== drug: haloperidol
name: Haloperidol
class: Typical (first-generation) antipsychotic — butyrophenone
group: Psychiatric
nemlit: B (tablet 1.5, 5 mg; injection 5 mg/mL); decanoate depot
forms: Tablet 1.5 mg, 5 mg; injection 5 mg/mL; decanoate depot 50 mg/mL
moa: Potent D2 receptor antagonist in mesolimbic pathway (reduces positive symptoms); low anticholinergic/sedative activity.
uses:
- Schizophrenia and acute psychosis; acute agitation/aggression (rapid tranquillisation, often with promethazine)
- Delirium; nausea/vomiting and agitation in palliative care; mania
dose:
- Psychosis: 1.5–5 mg two to three times daily (usual 5–10 mg/day; max 20 mg)
- Acute agitation: 5 mg IM (± promethazine 25–50 mg IM), repeat after 30–60 min if needed
- Delirium/palliative nausea: 0.5–1.5 mg at night or twice daily
ci:
- Comatose states, CNS depression, Parkinson's disease, dementia with Lewy bodies, QT prolongation
se:
- Extrapyramidal side effects: acute dystonia, akathisia, parkinsonism, tardive dyskinesia
- Neuroleptic malignant syndrome (fever, rigidity, autonomic instability, raised CK)
- QT prolongation, hyperprolactinaemia, sedation, postural hypotension
ddi:
- QT-prolonging drugs (amiodarone, erythromycin, quinine, ondansetron) :: Torsades :: Avoid; ECG
- Metoclopramide :: Additive EPS/NMS :: Avoid
- Carbamazepine / rifampicin :: Reduced haloperidol levels :: Increase dose
- Levodopa :: Mutual antagonism :: Avoid
- Alcohol / CNS depressants :: Sedation :: Caution
food:
- Alcohol :: Additive sedation :: Avoid
- Tea/coffee (with liquid forms) :: May precipitate drug :: Do not mix oral liquid with tea/coffee
preg: Use if needed; EPS in neonate if used late.
lact: Compatible (monitor infant).
monitor:
- EPS, temperature, ECG (high doses/IV), weight
advice:
- Do not stop suddenly; adherence prevents relapse
- Report stiffness, restlessness, abnormal movements, fever with rigidity
- Avoid alcohol; may cause drowsiness
storage: Below 25°C, protect from light.
pearls:
- Acute dystonia → benzhexol/biperiden; NMS → stop drug, cool, supportive, dantrolene/bromocriptine

=== drug: chlorpromazine
name: Chlorpromazine
class: Typical antipsychotic — phenothiazine (low potency)
group: Psychiatric
nemlit: A (tablet 25, 100 mg; injection 25 mg/mL)
forms: Tablet 25 mg, 100 mg; injection 25 mg/mL (2 mL)
moa: D2 antagonist with marked H1, α1 and muscarinic blockade (sedating, hypotensive, anticholinergic).
uses:
- Schizophrenia and acute psychosis (especially when sedation needed); mania
- Intractable hiccups; nausea/vomiting
dose:
- Psychosis: 25–100 mg three times daily or 75–300 mg at night (max 1 g/day)
- Acute agitation: 25–50 mg IM (deep), repeat after 1 h if needed
ci:
- Bone marrow depression, CNS depression, closed-angle glaucoma, phaeochromocytoma
se:
- Sedation, postural hypotension, anticholinergic effects (dry mouth, constipation, urinary retention)
- Photosensitivity and skin pigmentation, EPS, cholestatic jaundice, agranulocytosis, weight gain, lowered seizure threshold
ddi:
- Antihypertensives / alcohol :: Hypotension :: Monitor
